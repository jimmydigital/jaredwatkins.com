STEP 5  Three different 'useful lives', and why people argue
==============================================================================

  'Useful life' is three questions wearing one coat. Same chip, same decay
  curve, three answers - which is how Burry and Meta both get to be right.

    T_cash     revenue falls below cash opex        -> turn it off
    T_payback  cumulative margin repays capex       -> was it a good buy
    T_replace  a new chip in the SAME slot wins     -> swap it out

  chip        R0 $/hr   capex $   T_cash  T_payback  T_replace  peak margin
  V100           2.48    14,496     25.4        0.9       14.7      128,035
  A100           4.10    21,750     27.4        0.8        9.9      221,282
  H100           7.62    41,812     27.1        0.9        5.6      408,517
  H200           6.00    43,312     24.6        1.2        5.0      303,987
  B200           6.00    73,750     22.2        2.0        6.2      283,435
  B300           8.00    87,250     23.9        1.8         --      397,241

  peak margin = most cash the slot can ever produce, capex excluded.
  Where that is below capex, the purchase never repays itself.

STEP 6  The infrastructure ceiling
==============================================================================
  tier 1  2015-2020        5-15 kW/rack  $  6.0M/MW  air + containment
           hosts: V100;A100(partial)
  tier 2  2020-2023       15-40 kW/rack  $ 10.0M/MW  dense air + rear-door HX
           hosts: A100;H100;H200
  tier 3  2023-2026     100-140 kW/rack  $ 20.0M/MW  direct-to-chip liquid
           hosts: GB200 NVL72;GB300 NVL72;Vera Rubin NVL144
  tier 4  2027-2028     200-600 kW/rack  $ 28.0M/MW  DLC at scale / hybrid
           hosts: Rubin Ultra NVL576
  tier 5  2029+            600+ kW/rack  $ 35.0M/MW  immersion / unknown
           hosts: unknown

  Two chip generations per tier, three at the outside. Inside a tier the next
  chip is a forklift swap and the old one gets evicted on chip economics alone.
  Across a tier boundary the incumbent gets a stay of execution, because the
  challenger has to fund a building before it can beat anything.

  Retrofit where the shell allows it: ~$2M/MW, versus $11M+/MW greenfield
  liquid-cooled. That gap is the entire reason tier 2 hardware kept earning
  through 2025 while tier 3 hardware was already shipping.


==============================================================================
STEP 7  The formula
==============================================================================

  Put it together. Value of a deployed slot at age t:

      V(t) = INT[t..T*] ( R0*e^(-lam_r*s) - C_pwr - C_opex ) ds  +  P0*e^(-lam_p*T*)

      lam_r = 0.11/yr   rental revenue decay      (market-median basis;
                           0.06 to 0.25 depending on which market you measure)
      lam_p = 0.24/yr   resale value decay        (fit, R2=0.82)
      T*                   set by REPLACEMENT, not wear, and gated by the
                           infrastructure tier the slot belongs to

  Straight-line depreciation over L years implies a constant 25%/yr
  writedown. The fitted revenue curve writes down 11% in year one and
  20% by the end of year two. Book value and economic value diverge
  fastest in exactly the years an operator is most levered.

  chip           yr1     yr2     yr3     yr4     yr5     yr6   basis
  revenue         89      80      71      64      57      51   fitted
  resale          78      61      48      38      30      23   fitted
  SL 4yr          75      50      25       0       0       0   accounting
  SL 6yr          83      67      50      33      17       0   hyperscaler
