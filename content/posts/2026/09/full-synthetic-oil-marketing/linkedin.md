Full synthetic oil. Genuine leather. All natural food. Three labels that sound like a promise and mean almost nothing.

In 1999 Mobil sued Castrol for calling a base oil made from ordinary refined crude "full synthetic." The ad regulator sided with Castrol: since the crude goes through a chemical process on its way out of the refinery, it counts. Every major refiner switched to the cheaper process within a few years, and the American Petroleum Institute quietly dropped the word "synthetic" from its own standards because it couldn't certify a marketing claim anymore.

Leather runs the same play. Full-grain and top-grain are the real hide. A couple of grades below that sits "genuine leather," which sounds reassuring and actually means processed scraps from the bottom of the tannery. The FDA has never legally defined "natural" on a food label either, so corn syrup and genetically modified ingredients both clear the bar under the informal standard.

None of that would matter much right now except the crude underneath the oil industry version just had a brutal month. War in the Middle East has disrupted the Strait of Hormuz and knocked out Saudi Arabia's backup pipeline, and Costco is now rationing Kirkland Signature and Mobil 1 as prices push toward $60 a jug.
