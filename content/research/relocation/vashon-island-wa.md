Vashon-Maury Island is a rural island in central Puget Sound between West Seattle and Tacoma. It is unincorporated King County — there is no city government — and it has no bridge: every person, vehicle, and pallet arrives by boat. This profile is a sub-area companion to the [Seattle, WA profile]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}); state-level and metro-level material (taxes, firearms law, regional employers, major hospitals, pro sports) is covered there and only summarized here. Four island-specific topics get their own sections after the standard criteria: [ferries](#ferries--getting-on-and-off-the-island), [freight and prefab delivery](#shipping-construction-materials--prefab-structures), [ADU zoning](#adu-zoning--permitting), and the [farmers market and local resources](#farmers-market--local-food).

## Cost of Living

Vashon is priced like close-in King County despite being rural and ferry-dependent.

**Housing (2026):**
- Zillow typical home value: \$876,082 as of September 2026, down 2.2% year over year — above the King County figure of \$824,870 on the same index
- Median list price: \$964,500 (Zillow, September 2026); 59 homes for sale
- Median price of homes closed in May 2026: \$915,000, with 20 closings that month (NWMLS data via a local brokerage)
- 2024 full-year median sale price: \$847,500, an island record at the time (NWMLS via Windermere Vashon)
- Median gross rent: \$1,444/month in the Census Bureau's 2023 ACS 5-year estimate; the rental pool is small and the figure lags the market

**vs. coastal SC baseline:** A typical Vashon home costs roughly 2.7x the ~\$320K Myrtle Beach-area median, and slightly more than Seattle itself. Washington's lack of a state income tax applies here as it does in Seattle.

**Island cost drivers not captured in an index:**
- Ferry fares on every off-island car trip (\$25.65 round trip for a standard vehicle and driver, more in summer — see [Ferries](#ferries--getting-on-and-off-the-island))
- Delivery surcharges on bulk and building materials, and contractor travel time billed against ferry waits
- Septic and water costs: almost all homes are on septic, and the county puts a replacement septic system at \$40,000–50,000
- Thin sales volume (about 20 closings in a strong month) means prices swing and choice is limited

## Demographics & Trends

- **Population:** 11,055 at the 2020 Census (Vashon CDP, which covers both Vashon and Maury islands)
- **Long-run trend:** slow growth — 9,309 (1990), 10,123 (2000), 10,624 (2010), 11,055 (2020)
- **Age:** median age about 53 at the 2020 Census; 3,252 residents (29%) were 65 or older. The share under 18 fell from 20% in 2010 to 16% in 2020
- **Income and education:** median household income \$120,571 (ACS 2024 5-year); 51.9% of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher (ACS 2023)
- **Housing tenure:** 4,717 households at the 2020 Census; one data aggregator citing ACS puts homeownership at about 81%

The island is aging and getting more expensive at the same time. Vashon HouseHold, the island's affordable-housing nonprofit, calls the result the "vanishing island effect": workers who staff island businesses and services increasingly cannot live there. At a March 2026 housing forum nearly everyone in the audience said they knew someone who had been forced to leave.

## Crime

Policing is by the King County Sheriff's Office (KCSO); Vashon is part of the southwest precinct along with White Center and Skyway. KCSO told an April 2025 community forum that the island has **two dedicated deputies on duty 24 hours a day**, that crime over the prior five years had changed little, and that the island continues to see low crime relative to the rest of the county. Anyone arrested is transported off-island for booking.

Caveats: KCSO was under county budget pressure through the 2026–27 budget cycle (minimum staffing was cut and then restored in other unincorporated precincts in 2025), and islanders have raised deputy visibility as a recurring complaint. CDP-level FBI-style rate comparisons were not located for this review; treat "low crime" as the sheriff's characterization rather than a computed rate.

## Major Employers & Tech Ecosystem

There is no meaningful on-island tech ecosystem and no large employer. Island employment is schools, grocery and retail, health care, construction and trades, farms, arts organizations, and a handful of small manufacturers (the Beachcomber identifies medical-model maker Sawbones as an island producer). Most professional households either commute to Seattle or Tacoma by ferry or work remotely.

For the regional job market — Amazon, Microsoft, Boeing, UW, and the startup scene — see the [Seattle profile]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}). The practical question for Vashon is commute tolerance: the passenger-only water taxi reaches downtown Seattle in about 22 minutes but runs weekdays only, and the car ferry lands in West Seattle, not downtown.

## Small Business Climate

State taxes are identical to Seattle's: no personal or corporate income tax, the gross-receipts B&O tax, and a high sales tax. What differs is local government:

- **No city layer.** Vashon businesses deal with King County, not the City of Seattle, so Seattle's JumpStart payroll tax and city-specific labor ordinances do not apply.
- **King County unincorporated-area minimum wage (2026):** \$18.32/hour for employers with 15 or fewer workers and under \$2 million in annual revenue; \$19.82/hour for larger or higher-revenue employers.
- **Permitting** runs through the King County Department of Local Services; on-site inspections are batched (fire inspections for Vashon-Maury occur only on Thursdays).

The island market is small (about 11,000 residents plus summer visitors) and the cost of getting goods and staff across the water is the defining constraint. The island's only national chain restaurant, a Subway, was reported closed in August 2026.

## Utilities & Infrastructure

### Power

Puget Sound Energy (PSE) supplies the island — not Seattle City Light — over four distribution circuits, with power arriving across the water from both the west and the east. Outages are a fact of island life: it is a heavily forested rural grid with overhead lines, and windstorms routinely take out large sections. A February 2025 windstorm cut power to the entire island, with roughly 5,000 islanders still out hours later. PSE has been installing covered "tree wire" on the island since 2025 to reduce branch-related faults, and its wildfire risk model flags parts of the south end as areas where summer public-safety power shutoffs are possible.

A standby generator or battery backup should be treated as standard equipment, not an upgrade.

### Water

All drinking water on the island comes from local precipitation — there is no pipeline from the mainland. The EPA designated the Vashon-Maury Island aquifer system a **sole source aquifer** in 1994. Supply is fragmented:

- 23 Group A and 124 Group B public water systems operate on the island, according to King County
- Seven larger purveyors (Water District 19, Heights Water, and others) serve most users
- More than 1,000 private wells exist, and little is systematically known about their condition

**Water District 19**, which serves about 1,500 connections including the town core, kept a moratorium on new water shares from 1996 until 2023. When it ended, the district issued 28 new shares by lottery in May 2023 to 14 applicants — about a third of those who applied. The district has since been debating how to prioritize and police unused shares. **Whether a specific parcel has, or can get, water is the first due-diligence question for any purchase or building plan.**

### Sewer

Most island properties use on-site septic systems regulated by Public Health – Seattle & King County. Parts of the shoreline (Quartermaster Harbor and several other areas) are in a state-mandated **Marine Recovery Area**, where septic systems must be professionally inspected every year (the county puts this at about \$435 per year). The county has proposed expanding the MRA to all shoreline properties; the Board of Health vote on that update has been pushed to 2027.

### Internet

Provider availability and speeds vary by road and were not verified against a current source in this review. Check serviceability at the specific address before committing — this matters more than usual on an island with a high remote-work share.

## Environmental & Natural Hazard Profile

- **Earthquake and isolation:** Vashon shares the Puget Sound seismic exposure described in the [Seattle profile]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}) (Seattle Fault, Cascadia subduction zone). The island-specific risk is isolation: if ferry docks are damaged, there is no road out. The 2016 Cascadia Rising exercise practiced exactly this — the National Guard landed trucks from a barge onto the beach at Jensen Point to simulate resupply. VashonBePrepared is the island's volunteer preparedness coalition.
- **Windstorms and outages:** the most frequent hazard in practice (see Power).
- **Legacy soil contamination:** Vashon-Maury Island is among the areas most affected by the **Tacoma Smelter Plume** — arsenic and lead deposited over roughly 1,000 square miles by the former Asarco copper smelter in Ruston, which closed in 1986. Contamination is heaviest toward the south end and Maury Island. The state and county run free soil testing and a yard cleanup program for eligible Vashon-Maury properties. Test soil before buying, gardening, or disturbing ground for construction.
- **Wildfire:** the fire chief has characterized the chance of a large-scale wildfire as low but a smaller localized fire as a real possibility; smoke from regional fires affects the island as it does Seattle.
- **Aquifer limits:** a rain-fed, patchy aquifer is vulnerable to over-pumping and, near the shoreline, saltwater intrusion.

## Long-Term Growth Limiting Factors

1. **Ferry dependence.** Washington State Ferries has run the Triangle route on reduced two-boat schedules repeatedly because of vessel and crew shortages, and replacement of the aging Fauntleroy terminal will require a construction-period schedule. Every aspect of island life is exposed to WSF's fleet and funding problems.
2. **Water.** A sole-source aquifer and a district that rationed connections for 27 years cap how much the island can grow.
3. **Zoning.** Almost all of the island is rural-zoned at one home per 2.5 to 10 acres, and state ADU liberalization mostly does not reach it.
4. **Housing cost and workforce loss.** Median prices near \$900K with median age in the 50s is not a self-sustaining labor market for schools, clinics, and trades.
5. **Health care access.** No hospital and no emergency department; a public hospital district subsidizes urgent care (see [Island-Specific Resources](#island-specific-resources)).
6. **Septic regulation.** Expanding shoreline inspection rules and \$40,000–50,000 replacement costs weigh on older waterfront properties.

## Firearms & Self-Defense Laws

Washington state law governs and is covered in the [Seattle profile]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}): concealed carry requires a CPL, magazines over 10 rounds are banned, universal background checks apply, the state has an ERPO law, and a permit-to-purchase requirement takes effect May 1, 2027. The difference from Seattle is local: Vashon is unincorporated, so there is no city government layered on top of state and county law. The island has one member-based gun club, the Vashon Sportsmen's Club (see [Shooting Ranges & Training Facilities](#shooting-ranges--training-facilities)); no public-access range was found.

Compared with the coastal SC baseline (permitless carry, no magazine limits, no red flag law), Washington is a major step toward restriction regardless of which part of King County one lives in.

## Relocation Factors

**Strengths**
- Genuinely rural — farms, forest, beaches — within about 20 minutes by boat of a tier-1 metro
- No state income tax; no city government or city taxes
- Strong civic fabric for its size: a volunteer-run tool library, a thrift store that funds island health care, a public hospital district, community radio, a land trust that has protected more than 1,600 acres
- Working local food system with a weekly market and a mapped network of farm stands
- Mild maritime climate

**Weaknesses**
- Housing costs equal to or above Seattle's, with far fewer listings
- Everything depends on the ferry: commuting, medical appointments, deliveries, contractors, evacuation
- Frequent power outages; water and septic are the owner's problem
- No hospital; limited specialty care; aging population
- Building anything is slower and more expensive than on the mainland (see the sections below)
- Washington's firearms and regulatory trajectory, as in Seattle

**Verdict:** Vashon suits someone who wants land, a small-town community, and proximity to Seattle, who works remotely or can tolerate a boat commute, and who is prepared to run their own utilities and wait on ferries. It is not a low-cost alternative to Seattle — it is a different way to spend Seattle-level money. For a build-your-own-compound plan (ADU, shop, prefab structures), the binding constraints are lot size under rural zoning, water availability, septic capacity, and ferry logistics, in roughly that order.

## Ferries & Getting On and Off the Island

There are three ways on and off, from two terminals. Schedules change seasonally; the figures below are from the fall 2026 timetables (in effect through December 26, 2026) and should be checked against the live schedule before planning around a specific sailing.

### North end: Fauntleroy–Vashon–Southworth "Triangle" (WSF, vehicles and passengers)

- Connects the north-end Vashon terminal with Fauntleroy (West Seattle) and Southworth (Kitsap Peninsula)
- **Crossing time:** about 20 minutes direct to Fauntleroy; sailings routed via Southworth take about 45 minutes
- **Frequency:** roughly 30 sailings a day in each direction on the three-boat schedule, starting around 4–5 a.m. and running past midnight
- **Vessels on the fall 2026 schedule:** Kittitas, Cathlamet, Issaquah
- **No vehicle reservations** — first come, first served. Commute-direction waits (eastbound mornings, westbound afternoons and evenings) can run multiple sailings
- **Reliability:** the route drops to a two-boat schedule when a vessel or crew is unavailable; it ran that way through November 8, 2026 while the Kittitas was in maintenance. The route carries about 2.3 million riders a year

### South end: Point Defiance–Tahlequah (WSF, vehicles and passengers)

- Connects the south-end Tahlequah terminal with Point Defiance in Tacoma
- **Crossing time:** about 15 minutes
- **Frequency:** 20 sailings each direction daily on the fall 2026 schedule; first boat 5:05 a.m. from Point Defiance (weekdays), last boat 10:55 p.m. from Tahlequah
- **Vessel:** Chetzemoka (single boat — any breakdown suspends the route)
- This is the practical route for anything coming from Tacoma, the Port of Tacoma, or I-5 from the south

### Passenger-only: King County Water Taxi (Vashon terminal – Pier 50, downtown Seattle)

- **Crossing time:** about 22 minutes on the *Sally Fox*
- **Schedule:** 10 round trips Monday–Friday — morning, midday, and afternoon/evening commuter sailings, with the first departure from Seattle at 5:30 a.m. Midday sailings are state-funded through at least June 2027
- **No weekend service as of November 2026.** A one-year Saturday pilot and a "Summer Sundays" pilot both ended October 9, 2026
- **Fares:** \$7 each way for adults (\$6 with ORCA); 18 and under ride free
- King County Metro routes 118 and 119 connect the terminals with the town core and are timed to both the water taxi and WSF

### Fares (WSF)

Vashon routes are **round trip, collected only on the way to the island** — leaving Vashon is free at the tollbooth.

| Fare (round trip, effective May 1, 2026) | Price |
|---|---|
| Passenger | \$7.60 |
| Vehicle under 14 ft and driver | \$20.25 |
| Standard vehicle (under 22 ft) and driver | \$25.65 |

- A peak-season surcharge applies to full-fare vehicles from May 1 through September 30
- Riders 18 and under travel free; multi-ride cards discount frequent travel
- A 3% surcharge applies to credit and debit card purchases (since March 1, 2026)
- Vehicles 22 ft and longer pay a length-based fare that is not sold online

## Shipping Construction Materials & Prefab Structures

There is no freight dock, rail, or bridge. Building materials reach Vashon three ways: bought on-island, trucked over on a state ferry, or landed by barge.

### On-island supply

- **Island Home Center & Lumber** in the town core (17633 97th Place SW) is the island's lumberyard and hardware store, and rents some heavy equipment.
- The nearest big-box stores are the Home Depots in Gig Harbor and on Delridge Way SW in Seattle; United Rentals and Home Depot rental yards in Gig Harbor and Tacoma are the usual sources for larger machines. One islander's account describes renting a 50-ft scissor lift and a neighbor a 25-ton excavator from the mainland, with ferry timing as the main coordination problem.
- Mainland aggregate and landscape suppliers will deliver but add an island fee — one supplier estimates \$100–400 per truckload on top of normal delivery.

### By state ferry (the default)

Commercial trucks ride the same WSF boats as cars. The rules that matter:

- **Fares are by overall length**, including anything towed. **Loads wider than 8 ft 6 in pay double** the length-based fare.
- **A Vehicle Travel Request form must be approved in advance** if the vehicle or load is any of: over 80,000 lb gross, wider than 8 ft 6 in, taller than 13 ft 4 in, or under 14 in of ground clearance. WSF asks for two business days to process (email wsfinfo@wsdot.wa.gov).
- **Modular homes are accepted** — haulers must also submit WSF's Modular Home Measurement Diagram with the request.
- **Weight postings at the Vashon terminals** (both routes) limit single-unit trucks and concrete mixers to 29 tons (5-axle), 31 tons (6-axle), and 33 tons (7-axle). The postings do not apply to semi-trailers or truck-and-trailer combinations.
- **Deck clearance varies by vessel** — from 13 ft 6 in to 16 ft across the fleet. The vessel assigned to a sailing is shown on the schedule; confirm clearance for the specific boat.
- **Low tides matter.** Extreme low tides make the loading ramps steep at Vashon, Fauntleroy, Southworth, and the Point Defiance–Tahlequah run. WSF asks long trucks, low-clearance rigs, and loads with long rear overhang to avoid those windows, and trucks with under 14 in of clearance may be refused.
- **No reservations and no guaranteed space.** WSF asks oversize loads to travel outside commute peaks, and state highway curfews for oversize rigs apply on the ferry too.

**Precedent for prefab by ferry:** The Lodges on Vashon — 16 free-standing modular units of about 570 sq ft each — were factory-built and brought to the island by ferry in 2015. The project's builders cited fewer crew trips and less material shipping as the main savings of building off-site.

**Practical sizing:** a structure that travels as a legal-width load (8 ft 6 in or less — most park models, container-based units, panelized kits, and flat-packed prefab) rides as an ordinary long vehicle at single fare with no special approval beyond height and weight checks. Anything wider is still possible but needs the request form, pays double, and should be scheduled mid-day on a favorable tide.

*Policy risk:* ferry operators can and do change oversize policy. Pierce County's separate ferry system (Anderson and Ketron islands — not Vashon) stopped accepting modular homes altogether, citing deck space and load balance. Confirm WSF's current position before ordering a factory-built home.

### By barge or landing craft

For loads too large or heavy for the ferry, for waterfront sites with poor road access, or for moving several truckloads at once, marine contractors will deliver directly to a beach or bulkhead:

- **Waterfront Construction** (Seattle) runs material barges, crane barges, and landing craft across Puget Sound. Its published fleet ranges from a 25 × 75 ft ramp barge with 60-ton capacity and a 12 × 27 ft movable ramp up to a 150 × 55 ft crane barge rated at 1,000 tons.
- **TE Walrath Marine** (Tacoma) operates a ramp barge from a loading dock in the Port of Tacoma and lists mobile homes, excavators, and quarry rock among its typical cargo.

Barge delivery is tide-dependent, needs a suitable landing site, and may trigger shoreline permitting. No pricing was verified for this review; expect a mobilization charge that makes it uneconomic for a single small load.

## ADU Zoning & Permitting

Vashon has no zoning code of its own. The **King County Code (K.C.C. 21A.08.030)** applies, permits come from the **King County Department of Local Services, Permitting Division**, and septic and well approvals come from **Public Health – Seattle & King County**. The state's recent ADU liberalization is aimed at urban growth areas; on Vashon, the rural-area rules below control.

### What is allowed

| Topic | Rural-area rule (applies to most of Vashon) |
|---|---|
| Number | One ADU per lot |
| Attached ADU | Allowed. Must share a common wall and read as one structure; a connecting breezeway counts only if under 10 ft long, fully enclosed, and heated |
| Detached ADU | Allowed only if the lot has a single primary dwelling **and** meets the minimum lot area for its zone — or, inside the Vashon Rural Town, is at least 3,200 sq ft |
| Size | Up to 1,000 sq ft heated plus 1,000 sq ft unheated. No limit if wholly within the existing home's basement or attic; a detached ADU's basement does not count |
| Height | Base height for the zone |
| Parking | No additional off-street parking required |
| Entrances | When attached, only one entrance may face the street |
| Agricultural (A) zones | Attached only, and only accessory to commercial agriculture on the site |
| Forest (F) zone | Prohibited |
| Critical areas | Detached ADUs are not allowed within critical area buffers; attached additions may be |

**Minimum lot area for a detached ADU, by zone:**

| Zone | Minimum lot |
|---|---|
| RA-2.5 | 1.875 acres |
| RA-5 | 3.75 acres |
| RA-10 | 7.5 acres |
| RA-20 | 15 acres |
| Rural Town (Vashon town core) | 3,200 sq ft |

The county's permit FAQ also describes an exception allowing a detached ADU on an RA-5 lot of at least 2.5 acres if the owner buys one Transferable Development Right; the March 2026 ADU handout does not mention it, so confirm it is still available.

**Other conditions**
- A **notice on title** identifying the unit as accessory must be recorded before the permit is approved.
- **Owner occupancy:** the county's March 2026 ADU handout no longer lists an owner-occupancy requirement, and a 2025 county presentation on Vashon reported it had been removed. The county's online permit FAQ still shows the older owner-occupancy language — confirm with Permitting.
- If a detached ADU is later split onto its own lot, neither lot can add another detached ADU unless it is at least twice the zone minimum.
- Design should be compatible with the main house; the county recommends (but does not require) a siting study to limit privacy and view impacts.
- New roofs over 600 sq ft need a solar-ready zone.

### What actually constrains an ADU on Vashon

In practice the zoning table is the easy part. A county planner's Vashon presentation listed the approvals to line up *before* applying:

1. **Critical area designation** — wetlands, steep slopes, and their buffers remove a lot of buildable area on island parcels.
2. **Septic approval** — the existing system must be shown adequate for the added bedrooms or be expanded. Public Health issued a revised ADU septic memo for applications received after April 1, 2025.
3. **Water approval** — proof of an adequate source. In Water District 19 this can mean needing an additional water share; on a well, it means demonstrating capacity.
4. **Shoreline review** for waterfront parcels.
5. **Fire access and flow** — fire sprinklers are required for many Vashon projects because of limited water for firefighting.

County staff put ADU permitting alone at **about three to six months**, and a pre-construction meeting is required once the permit issues. Applications go through MyBuildingPermit.com (King County → Building → Single Family Residential → New Construction → Accessory Dwelling Unit).

### What is not allowed, and what may change

- **RVs, tiny houses on wheels, and park-model trailers generally cannot be legally occupied as dwellings on private property** under current county code. A prefab unit becomes an ADU only when it is permitted as a building on a foundation with approved water and septic.
- **Pilot proposal:** Vashon HouseHold is preparing a three-year pilot that would allow regulated occupancy of movable units with sanitation plans, fire spacing, and rent caps, prioritizing nonprofit and land-trust owners. It had not been adopted as of the March 2026 forum.
- **Pre-approved plans:** King County is developing a catalog of pre-approved ADU designs at the request of the Vashon-Maury Community Council's affordable housing committee.
- **Water-saving systems:** the county is developing policy to allow greywater systems and low-water composting toilets in ADUs, aimed at the island's aquifer limits.

## Farmers Market & Local Food

**Vashon Island Farmers Market**
- **Operator:** Vashon Island Growers Association (VIGA), a nonprofit that began in the 1980s
- **When:** Saturdays, 10 a.m.–3 p.m.; the 2026 season ran May 2 through September 26
- **Where:** the Village Green, 17519 Vashon Highway SW, in the center of town
- **What:** island-grown produce, flowers, plant starts, prepared food, artisans, and live music; leashed dogs welcome
- **Payment:** EBT tokens and SNAP Market Match are accepted at the market for eligible foods; **VIGA Farm Bucks** — scrip issued in \$2 increments through VIGA's Food Access Partnership — spend like cash at the market and at participating farm stands

**Farm stands.** The more distinctive feature is the island's network of unattended roadside farm stands. VIGA publishes a **farm stand map**, updated through the growing season, online and in print around town, marking which stands take Farm Bucks. For a resident this functions as a distributed, seven-day market.

**Beyond the market.** VIGA also runs a food preservation program that processes donated island produce, a "Get Growing" workshop series for home gardeners, a mentoring network for farmers, shared equipment, and work on island grain infrastructure. In 2026 it added a project coordinator funded by a USDA Farmers Market Promotion Program grant to open more sales channels for growers.

**Food hub.** The historic fruit barreling plant at the Mukai farm is being restored as a food hub with three or four tenant food businesses plus a community kitchen, with operation expected to begin in November 2026.

**Groceries.** Two full-service grocery stores serve the island — Vashon Thriftway and Vashon Market IGA — along with Minglement, a natural-foods store inside the coffee roasterie building. The Vashon-Maury Community Food Bank moved into a new, larger facility in 2026.

## Island-Specific Resources

Things that exist on Vashon because it is an island and has to supply them itself:

**Reuse and sharing**
- **Granny's Attic** — the island's thrift store, operating since 1975 as a nonprofit that grants its profits to island health and wellness organizations: about \$250,000 in the most recent year reported, and more than \$2.2 million since 1976. Ten staff and about 145 volunteers. Located in Vashon Market Plaza.
- **Vashon Tool Library** — volunteer-run lending library of more than 1,500 tools (construction, woodworking, plumbing, electrical, garden, mechanic's), next to Open Space for Arts & Community. In 2024 it made over 3,000 loans to 400+ active members. The same nonprofit runs the **Fix-It Café** repair events and the **Vashon MakerSpace**.
- **Musical instrument lending library** (Vashon Events) and the **Vashon Library** (King County Library System) round out a formal "library coalition."

**Health care without a hospital**
- **Vashon Health Care District** — a public hospital district created by voters in 2019 (about 70% approval) and funded by property tax. It does not run a hospital; it funds gaps.
- **Sea Mar Community Health Centers** — the island's primary care clinic, operating at Sunrise Ridge and building a new two-story clinic in the town core with dental and behavioral health, which Sea Mar said in July 2026 was on track to open in the fall.
- **DispatchHealth** — mobile urgent care that comes to the patient's home, seven days a week including holidays, under a district contract of roughly \$585,000 for 2026.
- **Mobile Integrated Health** — a Vashon Island Fire & Rescue program for post-hospital follow-up and non-emergency care at home.
- Also on island: Vashon Pharmacy, Vashon Natural Medicine, and a Neighborcare school-based clinic at Vashon High School. Emergencies go off-island by ferry or airlift; the regional Level I trauma center is in Seattle.

**Information and preparedness**
- **Voice of Vashon** (101.9 FM) — community radio, which also carries island alerts and storm information
- **The Vashon-Maury Island Beachcomber** (weekly newspaper) and *The Vashon Loop*
- **VashonBePrepared** — volunteer disaster-preparedness coalition tied to the island's Emergency Operations Center
- **Vashon-Maury Island Groundwater Protection Committee** — county-appointed volunteers who monitor wells and streams and run a private-well stewardship program

**Governance and land**
- **Vashon-Maury Community Council** — the advisory body that stands in for a city council in dealing with King County
- **Vashon Island Fire & Rescue**, **Vashon Park District**, **Vashon Island School District**, the Health Care District, and the water districts are separate special-purpose districts with their own boards
- **Vashon-Maury Island Land Trust** — has helped protect more than 1,600 acres since 1989, including Island Center Forest
- **Vashon HouseHold** — affordable-housing nonprofit and the main local voice on ADU and housing policy
- **Vashon Chamber of Commerce** — also runs the island's signature festivals

### Seasonal Recreation

- **Island Center Forest** — 440 acres of county forest with about 10 miles of multi-use trails for hiking, mountain biking, and riding.

### Shooting Ranges & Training Facilities

**Vashon Sportsmen's Club** is the island's gun club and the only shooting facility identified. It sits at the end of a long road beside the Vashon Cemetery and began as a gun club in 1928; the current club was formed in 1933. A local-history profile describes the grounds as having a fishing pond, a clubhouse, and "an elaborate, well-designed shooting range." The club is a member-based nonprofit social and recreational organization; its IRS filing reports 333 members.

- **Trap:** informal trapshooting on Wednesday afternoons for all experience levels, per the Beachcomber's coverage of the 2025 Turkey Shoot (32 competitors, 25 clay targets each, held before Thanksgiving).
- **Rimfire and shotgun:** taught at the club's summer Kids Camp (about 21 campers in 2023), which also covers archery, fishing, and camping skills.
- **Rifle and pistol:** a third-party range directory lists rifle shooting stations under shelters, but no club or news source reviewed confirms rifle or pistol hours, caliber limits, or whether non-members can shoot.
- **Other uses:** clubhouse rentals, scout and VFW meetings, family movie nights, a chili cook-off, and the annual all-island Kids' Trout Derby (May 17 in 2026).
- **Membership, dues, and guest policy:** not verified; the club's own site blocked automated retrieval. Contact details are on its website (vashonsportsmensclub.com).

No commercial or public-access range was found on the island. Metro-area ranges are in the Seattle profile, and Kitsap Peninsula facilities are reachable via the Southworth ferry. The club describes its mission as supporting sustainable hunting, fishing, and outdoor skills.

### Private Clubs

- **Vashon Island Golf & Country Club** (24615 75th Ave SW) is a nine-hole course dating to the 1920s with a swimming pool. Listings describe it variously as private and semi-private. The club's site lists a proprietary membership at \$319 per month plus a \$250 initiation fee and a \$750 owner's certificate fee.
- **Vashon Sportsmen's Club** is member-based; see Shooting Ranges & Training Facilities above.
- No yacht club, fraternal lodge, or other private social club was identified in the sources reviewed; absence was not verified.

### Home-Based & Personal Services

No single vetted directory covers island freelancers; most work comes by word of mouth, the community directory at vashon-maury.com, and national marketplaces. Marketplace listings mix on-island and off-island providers, so confirm where someone lives and whether ferry costs are billed.

- **Housekeeping:** Care.com listed about 60 housekeepers for Vashon, with an island average of roughly \$22 per hour as of June 2025 and individual rates from about \$20 to \$50. The Bearded Lady Housekeeping has operated on the island since 2007. Many listings are for providers based in Olalla or Federal Way.
- **Personal and private chefs:** Take a Chef lists chefs for Vashon, but they offer services anywhere in Washington, so they are likely off-island. A BBB listing shows Island Nest Nanny and Helper, based in Vashon, offering personal chef, organizing, and assistance services. Chef rates were not verified.
- **Pet and house sitting:** Joyful Pet Sitting and Susan's Pet & House Sitting are listed with Pet Sitters International, and several Rover sitters live on the island.
- **Handyman, landscaping, and trades:** Angi and HomeAdvisor list Vashon-area providers, though several handyman results are mainland firms. Islanders typically trade recommendations for plumbing, HVAC, gutter cleaning, and landscaping.

### Crime & Controversy — Notable Incidents

- **Ferry service cuts** — the Triangle route's repeated drops to two-boat service in recent years are the island's longest-running public controversy; islanders argue Vashon is disproportionately harmed because it has no road alternative.
- **Clinic instability** — the island's primary care clinic changed operators in 2020, and in 2022 Sea Mar announced it would leave after a dispute with the Health Care District before reversing course and staying as an independent, unsubsidized clinic.
- **Thunderbird Treatment Center** — the Seattle Indian Health Board's 92-bed inpatient substance use treatment center on Vashon Highway was slated to open in summer 2026.
- **Drug enforcement** — KCSO has announced arrests of suspected drug traffickers on the island following a multi-month investigation; addiction and recovery were the main topics at the April 2025 public safety forum.
