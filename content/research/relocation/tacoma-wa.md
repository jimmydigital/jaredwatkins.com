Tacoma is Washington's third-largest city, a port and university city on Commencement Bay about 30 miles south of Seattle. It shares Washington's state-level rules with the [Seattle, WA profile]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}) (no income tax, firearms law, regional employers), which are summarized here rather than repeated. This profile focuses on two sub-areas that behave very differently:

- **Proctor District** — the North End's walkable "urban village" around N 26th and N Proctor, with a historic theater, a Saturday farmers market, and some of the city's highest home prices.
- **West Slope** — the residential west side above Puget Sound (Titlow Park, Salmon Beach, the Narrows), generally quieter and more car-oriented, and partly inside the Tacoma Smelter Plume soil-contamination footprint.

Nearby sub-area: [Vashon Island]({{< relref "vashon-island-wa.md" >}}) is reachable by the Point Defiance–Tahlequah ferry from Tacoma's north end.

## Cost of Living

**Citywide:** Zillow put Tacoma's median home value at about \$498,000 in June 2026, while a broker site showed a median *listing* price near \$619,000 in September 2026 — the two measure different things (estimated value of all homes versus asking price of current listings). Redfin reported a November 2025 median sale price of about \$484,000. The coastal SC baseline is about \$320K, so Tacoma runs roughly 50–95% higher depending on the measure.

**Proctor District:** one local brokerage listed the 2026 Proctor median at \$741,000 (an older page from the same site shows \$707,000), and the wider North Tacoma median at \$650,000. Nearby: Stadium District \$1.25M, Old Town \$950K, Three Bridges \$768K, North Slope \$740K, UPS neighborhood \$610K. Proctor-area sales are competitive (a 99.7% sale-to-list ratio was reported). Housing stock is craftsman, Tudor and foursquare homes on tree-lined streets.

**West Slope:** sources conflict. Older figures put the median around \$578K–\$600K; a newer local brokerage figure is \$747,500. Redfin's neighboring "West End" area showed a \$649,000 listing price and "Northeast Tacoma" \$800,000. Treat West Slope as roughly \$600K–\$750K and verify against current MLS comps; water-view and Narrows-view homes sit above that.

**Sales tax:** Tacoma's combined rate is 10.5% (6.5% state plus 4.0% local) per most 2026 sources, though a few aggregators show lower figures (9.6%–10.3%); the state Department of Revenue lookup is authoritative. Groceries and prescriptions are exempt; Washington has no personal income tax but taxes gross receipts (B&O) and has a separate capital gains tax (see [Seattle]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}})).

**Rent and income:** Zillow's rent index was about \$1,753 in January 2026. Census-based figures show a citywide median household income of roughly \$84,000–\$86,000; the north Tacoma PUMA is higher (about \$94,600).

## Demographics & Trends

- **Population:** about 220,000 (ACS), with more recent estimates near 223,000–224,000; median age about 37.
- **Composition (ACS-based, approximate):** roughly 56% White, 10% Black, 13% Hispanic/Latino, 9% Asian; a diversity index of 63/100 against a state average of 55. Poverty rate about 12%; bachelor's degree or higher about 33.5%; homeownership about 56.5%.
- **Trend:** the five-year median home value rose roughly 70% while median income rose about 38%. Prices softened slightly in 2025–26 (Zillow −0.4% year over year in June 2026) even as Pierce County prices rose about 3% on a three-month basis.
- **Neighborhood contrast:** Proctor is an established, high-owner-occupancy North End district; the Hilltop neighborhood near the light-rail extension is a historically Black area where displacement has been a documented concern.

## Crime

- **Homicides:** 45 in 2022 (a record), 34 in 2023, and 22 in 2024; the city's long-run average is about 18 per year. A 2025 report noted 11 homicides in under five months, nearly double the same period of 2024, so the decline is not monotonic. (Another source in this research cites 27 for 2023; the 34 figure is the one used by the News Tribune and TPD list.)
- **Policing:** the Violent Crime Reduction Plan (2022–2025), built with UT San Antonio, uses hot-spot patrols; the city reported violent crime down 17.3% and total crime down 23.6% year to date at one 2025 briefing, while an independent evaluation described violent crime as still elevated against 2020 levels. The department's solve rate was about 67% for 2024.
- **Where:** crime concentrates in specific hot spots (the Tacoma Mall area saw a reported spike), not in Proctor or most of the West Slope, whose property crime is typical of an urban North End or residential west side — verify block-level data before buying.
- **Baseline comparison:** coastal SC baseline cities generally have lower violent-crime volatility; Tacoma's 2022 peak was the worst in decades.

## Major Employers & Tech Ecosystem

Employment is anchored by healthcare (MultiCare, CHI Franciscan/Virginia Mason Franciscan), Joint Base Lewis-McChord to the south, the Port of Tacoma and its logistics economy, the University of Washington Tacoma, the University of Puget Sound and Pacific Lutheran University, and Tacoma Public Utilities. Tech is a commuter story — Seattle and Bellevue employers are 30–45 miles north by I-5 or Sound Transit's Sounder commuter rail — rather than a local cluster. See [Seattle]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}) for the regional tech ecosystem.

## Small Business Climate

Washington taxes gross receipts through the B&O tax (rates vary by classification, roughly 0.138%–1.75% for most categories, with surcharges on some services) instead of an income tax, and sales tax applies to many services including construction, repair and cleaning. Proctor is the clearest small-business story: a curated retail core of independent boutiques, restaurants, a long-running farmers market whose vendors collectively generate millions of dollars of sales a year, and the Proctor Arts Fest (30th anniversary in August 2026). The city's makerspace and craft scene (see [Maker Spaces](#maker-spaces)) supports small-batch producers.

## Utilities & Infrastructure

### Power

Tacoma Public Utilities (a municipal, non-tax-funded utility) provides electricity to about 176,000 customers and water to about 102,000. Tacoma Power is roughly 91% hydroelectric and 9% biomass; the cited average residential rate is about 9.9 cents/kWh (older data) and TPU says its April 2026 rates remain among the most affordable in the region. TPU's long-term plan assumes increases of about 3.5% per year through 2028. Reliability: an average of about 314 minutes of outage per customer in 2021.

### Water

Tacoma Water is municipal; the system serves the city and surrounding areas. No source-water concerns specific to Proctor or West Slope were found, but see the soil-contamination note below.

### Sewer

City sewer and stormwater service (Environmental Services) is combined with TPU on one bill.

### Internet

Click! Network, run by Tacoma Power, is a municipal fiber/cable provider (about 23,000 internet customers in older data), alongside commercial carriers. Check address-level availability.

## Environmental & Natural Hazard Profile

- **Tacoma Smelter Plume:** the former Asarco copper smelter in Ruston (one of the first Superfund sites) deposited arsenic and lead across more than 1,000 square miles of the Puget Sound basin. EPA and Asarco replaced soil at about 2,436 properties in Ruston and North Tacoma. The state's follow-on program covers roughly 3,900 homes in West Tacoma outside the Superfund boundary; yards qualify at a parcel average above 100 ppm arsenic or 500 ppm lead. **Relevance:** Proctor lies in the North Tacoma Superfund study area and West Slope in the West Tacoma program area, so ask whether a yard was sampled or cleaned and check Ecology's Dirt Alert map before buying.
- **Seismic and lahar:** Tacoma sits in the Cascadia subduction zone and the Puyallup/Mount Rainier lahar region; the lahar hazard concentrates in the Puyallup valley, not on the Proctor or West Slope uplands. Liquefaction risk is higher near the tideflats and waterfront (Salmon Beach is steep and bluff-adjacent).
- **Climate:** about 39 inches of rain a year, roughly three-quarters falling October–March; mild summers.

## Long-Term Growth Limiting Factors

- **Housing supply:** zoning reform (Home in Tacoma, HB 1110 middle housing) is expanding allowed density, but North End prices are already high and sale-to-list ratios near 100%.
- **Transit timeline:** the Tacoma Dome Link Extension to Seattle was delayed to 2035 in 2023 (some sources still cite earlier dates); the Hilltop T Line extension opened Sept. 16, 2023, and further extension to Tacoma Community College is planned by 2039.
- **Public safety perception:** violent crime remains elevated against pre-2020 levels.
- **Soil legacy:** the smelter plume affects real estate disclosure and yard work in the north and west parts of the city.

## Firearms & Self-Defense Laws

Washington state law governs and is covered in [Seattle]({{< relref "seattle-wa.md" >}}): concealed carry needs a CPL, magazines over 10 rounds are banned, universal background checks apply, the state has an ERPO law, and a permit-to-purchase requirement takes effect May 1, 2027. No Tacoma-specific carry ordinance was found. Compared with the coastal SC baseline (permitless carry, no magazine limits, no red flag law), Washington is substantially more restrictive. For ranges, see [Shooting Ranges & Training Facilities](#shooting-ranges--training-facilities).

## Relocation Factors

- **Proctor fits:** walkable errands, a year-round-ish farmers market, independent theater and bowling, good schools by local reputation (Washington Elementary and Mason Middle are in the district), and Link access in the Stadium District; trade-offs are price and tight inventory.
- **West Slope fits:** quieter streets, parks and water access (Titlow Park, the Narrows), more space per dollar than the North End, a car-dependent lifestyle; trade-offs are smelter-plume soil checks, steep lots and fewer walkable amenities.
- **ADUs:** Tacoma allows detached and attached ADUs citywide on lots that permit single-family development (since 2019 reforms). No off-street parking required; if parking is provided it must be in the rear and not remove required primary parking. No owner-occupancy requirement; state law (HB 1337) allows two ADUs per lot. Historic detached ADUs are capped at 1,000 sq ft (older rules: lesser of 1,000 sq ft, 15% of lot, or 85% of the main house — confirm current limits). Tacoma does not charge impact fees on housing, publishes pre-approved DADU plans, and North Slope historic-district homes face extra design review. Check the city's permit portal for current fees.
- **Schools:** Tacoma Public Schools; verify attendance zones at the address.
- **Getting around:** I-5 to Seattle (traffic-dependent), Sounder rail, the T Line light rail (Tacoma Dome to Hilltop, 11 stations), and the Point Defiance–Tahlequah ferry to Vashon.

### Bookstores

- Proctor retail includes **Teaching Toys & Books** and a Proctor sidewalk and book sale during Proctor Arts Fest. Larger independent bookstores were not verified in this round.

### Furniture Consignment

- Proctor secondhand shops include **Blue** (Goodwill's upscale boutique), **Bloomington Kids**, and **Megs & Mo Upscale Resale**. Tacoma Discovery Shop (American Cancer Society thrift) is also in the district.

### Healthcare

- **MultiCare Tacoma General Hospital** (Level II trauma) and **Mary Bridge Children's Hospital** (pediatric Level II) share a campus at 315 Martin Luther King Jr Way.
- **St. Joseph Medical Center** (Virginia Mason Franciscan Health) is a Level II trauma center about a mile away. The two hospitals ended their alternating trauma-duty arrangement on Nov. 17, 2024; each now provides trauma care 24/7.
- **Madigan Army Medical Center** at JBLM is a Level II trauma center that primarily serves military beneficiaries.

### Comedy Clubs

- **Tacoma Comedy Club** exists but its downtown location details are in flux in sources (relocation plus a reported second location on 6th Avenue); verify before visiting. Pride-month comedy events have been held at McMenamins Elks Temple's Spanish Ballroom.

### Catholic Churches

- Tacoma has about 12–14 parishes in the Archdiocese of Seattle. Closest to Proctor: **St. Patrick** (1123 N J St, Old Town) and **Holy Cross** (5510 N 44th St). On the west side: **St. Charles Borromeo** (7112 S 12th St), founded in 1956, with Spanish-language Mass. Holy Rosary's historic church was merged into St. Ann in 2021.

### Maker Spaces

- **FabLab Tacoma** — public woodshop and fabrication lab (laser cutter, CNC router, 3D printer, metalworking), open since 2012.
- **Tacoma Public Library Maker Labs** (including Tinker Time hours and a Memory Lab) and the **Tacoma Tool Library** at the Moore Library.
- **UW Tacoma Library Makerspace** is for UWT students, faculty and staff only.
- **Toolbox Learning Laboratories** offers sliding-scale community workshops.

### Seasonal Recreation

- Titlow Park and Beach (West Slope), Ruston Way waterfront, and Point Defiance Park and Zoo & Aquarium are the main outdoor anchors; Mount Rainier is about 90 minutes away. Details on specific trails and seasonal opening hours were not verified.

### Annual Festivals & Events

- **Proctor Arts Fest** — free street fair at N 26th and Proctor; 30th year on Aug. 1, 2026.
- **Proctor Farmers Market** — Saturdays at N 27th and Proctor/Madison, running late March through December with reduced winter dates; operating since 1994 per one source, with another dating the Tacoma farmers market to 1990.
- **Daffodil Festival** (parades in Tacoma, Puyallup, Sumner, Orting; Junior Daffodil Parade held in the Proctor District in some years).
- **Taste of Tacoma** moved in 2022 to the Washington State Fairgrounds as Taste Northwest.
- **Tacoma Pride** (second Saturday of July, Wright Park), **Art on the Ave**, **Tacoma Arts Month** (October), **Tacoma Film Festival**, and the **IN THE SPIRIT Arts Market & Northwest Native Festival** (August). MOSAIC (formerly Ethnic Fest) did not run in 2026.

### Tourism

- Pierce County visitor spending reached \$1.67 billion in 2022 (about 9.0 million visitors); in an earlier report Tacoma captured roughly 43–45% of county spending. Draws include the Museum District (Museum of Glass, Washington State History Museum, Tacoma Art Museum), Point Defiance, and Mount Rainier access. Tacoma's tourism growth was reported as lagging Seattle's after the pandemic.

### Event Venues

- **Tacoma Dome** (built 1983, renovated 2018), **Cheney Stadium**, **Pantages Theater** (1,273 seats, opened 1918), **Rialto Theater**, and **Theatre on the Square**, all run by Tacoma Venues & Events; plus **Blue Mouse Theatre** (Proctor) and **McMenamins Elks Temple** downtown.

### Sports Teams & Recreation Organizations

- **Tacoma Rainiers** (Triple-A, Mariners affiliate) at Cheney Stadium (about 6,500 seats per MiLB, with some older sources citing 9,600). The Tacoma Defiance (MLS Next Pro) now play at Starfire Stadium rather than Cheney. Local college teams include the UPS Loggers and PLU Lutes. Tacoma Stars (indoor soccer) play in Kent, not Tacoma.

### Motorsports

- No major track in Tacoma. **Pacific Raceways** in Kent (opened 1960; 2.25-mile road course plus a drag strip) is about 30 miles north.

### Shooting Ranges & Training Facilities

- No verified public-access range inside Tacoma was found in this round; one previously listed range (Bull's Eye) appears as permanently closed on a map site. Check current listings for Pierce County ranges before relying on any one of them, and see [Vashon Sportsmen's Club]({{< relref "vashon-island-wa.md" >}}) for the nearest member-based club with documented details.

### Crime & Controversy — Notable Incidents

- **2022 homicide record** (45) and the city's response through hot-spot policing; the police union has argued staffing was lower than in 2010.
- **Tacoma Mall area** crime spike reported alongside overall declines.
- **Hilltop Link delays and displacement** — the extension opened roughly a year late and over budget, and residents and businesses raised displacement and construction-impact concerns.
- **Holy Rosary Church** — an iconic I-5-visible church deemed unviable and merged into St. Ann in 2021.
- **Smelter-plume cleanup** — ongoing, area-by-area soil replacement.
