## Summary

Firefly Aerospace, Inc. (Nasdaq: FLY) is a Cedar Park, Texas–based space and defense technology company that designs, builds, and operates small-to-medium-lift orbital launch vehicles (Alpha, and Eclipse under co-development with Northrop Grumman), lunar landers (Blue Ghost), and in-space orbital transfer vehicles (Elytra). In March 2025 Firefly became the first commercial company in history to achieve a fully successful soft landing on the Moon, and the company completed one of the largest space-technology IPOs to date on August 7, 2025, closing its first trading day at roughly an $8.5 billion valuation. Firefly has also expanded into national-security sensing and software through its ~$855 million acquisition of missile-warning/ISR contractor SciTec (closed November 2025). The company's flagship Alpha rocket has a mixed flight record — including a September 2025 ground-test booster explosion — while Eclipse, a joint program with Northrop Grumman targeting NASA, national-security, and commercial medium-lift missions, has not yet flown as of this review.

## What It Is / How It Works

Firefly Aerospace positions itself as an "end-to-end responsive space" company spanning three product lines built on shared, flight-proven technologies: launch vehicles, lunar landers, and in-space orbital vehicles. Its Alpha rocket is a small-lift, carbon-composite launch vehicle powered by Firefly's own Reaver (first stage) and Lightning (second stage) engines, which use a patented tap-off cycle architecture; Alpha has flown seven times since September 2021 with a mixed record of full successes, partial failures, and one first-stage loss during ground testing (detailed below). Blue Ghost is Firefly's lunar lander line, built and operated under NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program; Blue Ghost Mission 1 delivered ten NASA science and technology instruments to Mare Crisium in March 2025, becoming the first fully successful commercial soft landing on the Moon. Elytra is Firefly's family of orbital transfer vehicles — Dawn, Dusk, and Dark, in ascending order of maneuverability, power, and payload capacity — designed for on-demand payload delivery, space domain awareness, imaging, and long-haul communications from LEO to lunar orbit and beyond; Elytra Dark is also used as the "space tug" that inserts Blue Ghost and companion payloads into lunar orbit on multi-spacecraft missions.

Eclipse, formerly known as the Medium Launch Vehicle (MLV), is a co-development program with Northrop Grumman (which invested $50 million into the program in May 2025) intended to fill the gap between Alpha's small-lift capability and larger vehicles. Eclipse combines a first stage built on Firefly's Alpha propulsion and carbon-composite-structure technology (scaled up, using Miranda and Vira tap-off-cycle engines) with Northrop Grumman's Antares flight-proven avionics and a larger 5.4-meter payload fairing; the same first stage is also being developed for Northrop Grumman's own Antares 330 rocket. Eclipse is designed to deliver roughly 16,300 kg to low Earth orbit or 3,200 kg to geosynchronous transfer orbit, and Firefly has explicitly positioned it as a candidate for the US Space Force's National Security Space Launch (NSSL) Phase 3 Lane 1 program, whose contract ceiling was raised from $5.6 billion to $17 billion in July 2026. As of this review, Eclipse had not yet conducted a first flight; Firefly's CEO has cited a "late 2026" target, with the company's Alpha Block 2 upgrade separately pushed to Q4 2026.

Beyond its core launch/lander/orbital-vehicle business, Firefly has moved into national-security sensing and software: in October 2025 it announced the acquisition of SciTec, a Princeton, NJ–based missile-warning, ISR, and space-domain-awareness contractor, for approximately $855 million (deal closed November 5, 2025); see the [SciTec entry]({{< relref "../drone-detection/hardware/scitec.md" >}}) for detail on that business, which continues to operate as a Firefly subsidiary. Firefly's lunar rover for its NASA-awarded Gruithuisen Domes mission is supplied by Blue Origin subsidiary [Honeybee Robotics]({{< relref "../robotics/space-robotics/honeybee-robotics.md" >}}) — notable as a case of a nominal launch-industry competitor (Blue Origin) supplying hardware to Firefly. In 2026 Firefly also acquired Space-ng, a vision-navigation and autonomous-guidance software company, to strengthen the autonomy stack on Blue Ghost and Elytra, and announced a collaboration with NVIDIA using a Jetson edge-compute module to process imagery on-orbit for Firefly's Ocula lunar imaging service.

## Notable Developments

- **2026-08-11:** Reported Q2 2026 results: record quarterly revenue of $117.7M (+659% YoY), backlog reported at ~$1.5B (per earnings-call coverage), reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $420–450M; also disclosed an underwritten public stock offering generating $181.6M in net proceeds and a headquarters campus expansion (more than quadrupled spacecraft cleanroom space).
- **2026-08-11:** Announced Alpha Block 2 debut pushed to Q4 2026.
- **2026-03-11:** Successfully launched Alpha Flight 7 ("Stairway to Seven") from Vandenberg SFB, delivering a Lockheed Martin demonstrator payload and validating Block II avionics/thermal-protection upgrades — Firefly's first Alpha launch since the April 2025 Flight 6 anomaly and the September 2025 ground-test explosion.
- **2025-11-05:** Completed acquisition of SciTec (missile warning/ISR/space-domain-awareness contractor) for ~$855M ($300M cash + $555M in Firefly stock).
- **2025-09-29:** An Alpha first-stage booster (intended for Flight 7) was destroyed in a ground acceptance-test explosion at Firefly's Briggs, TX facility; no injuries reported. Root cause was later attributed to "minute hydrocarbon contamination" from a process error during stage assembly, not a design flaw.
- **2025-08-07:** Firefly began trading on Nasdaq under "FLY" following an IPO priced at $45/share; shares closed the first day up more than 30% at $60.35, a reported ~$8.5B valuation.
- **2025-04-29:** Alpha Flight 6 suffered an in-flight anomaly (plume-induced heating that ruptured the booster during stage separation) and failed to reach its planned orbit while deploying a Lockheed Martin LM 400 tech-demo satellite.
- **2025-03-02:** Blue Ghost Mission 1 achieved a fully successful soft landing in Mare Crisium, the first by a commercial company, carrying ten NASA CLPS science/technology instruments; surface operations continued for 14 days (over 346 hours), including capturing a total solar eclipse and the first commercial HD video of a lunar sunset.
