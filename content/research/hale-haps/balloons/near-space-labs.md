## Summary

Near Space Labs is a Brooklyn, NY-based Earth-observation company that flies "Swift" — a 12-lb, zero-emission, helium-balloon-lifted autonomous imaging platform — at 60,000-85,000 ft to capture 7cm-resolution aerial imagery. Unlike the C-UAS-oriented balloon and airship platforms documented elsewhere on this site, Near Space Labs is built and sold specifically for civil Earth observation — primarily P&C insurance underwriting and claims, with secondary use in state/local government, utilities, forestry, and mapping — with no publicly disclosed defense or detection payload.

## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 2017 (per company's own November 2024 press release), based in Brooklyn, NY
- **Type:** Private company — stratospheric balloon imaging platform + data/analytics
- **Status:** Active; nationwide US deployment reached November 2024; continuing expansion as of 2025-2026
- **Platform ("Swift"):** 12-lb autonomous balloon-lifted imaging robot; operates at 60,000-85,000 ft; captures up to 1,000 sq km of imagery per flight (roughly equivalent to New York City's five boroughs); 7cm image resolution
- **Funding:** $20 million Series B (announced April 29, 2025), led by Bold Capital Partners (Peter Diamandis/XPRIZE-founded firm), with participation from USAA, Climate Capital, Gaingels, and River Park Ventures, alongside existing investors Crosslink Capital, Third Sphere, and Draper Associates. A company press release from November 2024 (pre-Series B) stated the company had raised $14.5 million to date; third-party aggregators (Tracxn) describe total funding as "more than $40 million" post-Series B — treat the aggregate figure as an estimate, since it isn't confirmed in a company-published source fetched in this review
- **Notable investor/board addition:** Tom Chi, a founding member of Google X and former head of Google's Project Loon (Google's own stratospheric-balloon internet program), joined as a strategic investor and board observer (announced October 2025)

## What It Is / How It Works

The Swift is a compact, carbon-fiber-airframe payload carried to the stratosphere by a weather balloon rather than powered flight. Near Space Labs describes launch as requiring less than 20 minutes and not needing an airfield, a fixed schedule, or direct ground access to the target survey area — flights can be launched hundreds of miles from where imagery is actually needed. Multiple Swifts can be coordinated in a single mission via the company's proprietary "StratoMap" flight-operations software to cover an area of hundreds of square miles. At mission end, the Swift descends by parachute to a designated recovery zone for reuse.

The company's pitch is that stratospheric altitude (65,000 ft cited as the reference operating altitude) sits above weather, air traffic, and most restricted airspace, letting a single flight combine a satellite's wide-area coverage with resolution closer to that of a drone or low-altitude fixed-wing aircraft — 7cm ground sample distance, which the company positions as matching or exceeding traditional manned aerial-survey quality. Every Swift carries an ADS-B transponder to broadcast position to air traffic control, and the company states every mission is coordinated directly with local ATC and the FAA.

The core commercial driver, per the company's own November 2024 release, is the deteriorating US home-insurance underwriting environment: net combined ratios for US homeowners insurers exceeded 110% in 2023 amid severe-weather losses, pushing carriers to retreat from high-risk states (Florida, California, Texas) and creating demand for more frequent, more granular property-risk imagery than legacy aerial-survey cycles provide. Near Space Labs positions quarterly nationwide imagery refreshes as a direct response to that gap. Beyond insurance, the company lists infrastructure monitoring, environmental/forestry monitoring, geospatial services, and "hosted payloads" (third-party sensor integration, described as onboarded "in weeks rather than years") among its named solution lines.
