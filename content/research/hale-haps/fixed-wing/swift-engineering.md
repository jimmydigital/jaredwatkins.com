## Summary

Swift Engineering is a diversified San Clemente, California aerospace and advanced-vehicle engineering firm (roots in open-wheel race car design) that is one of several US developers pursuing a solar-electric high-altitude, long-endurance (HALE) stratospheric aircraft, the Swift Ultra Long Endurance (SULE). SULE is one product line among many at Swift, which also does contract engineering and manufacturing for major defense primes (Northrop Grumman, Sikorsky, Lockheed Martin, Bell/DARPA) and builds its own VTOL and tactical UAS lines — so its HAPS work should be read as one part of a much broader unmanned-systems and aerospace-manufacturing business, not the company's sole focus.

## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 1983 (as Swift Racing Cars; renamed Swift Engineering in 1991 after acquisition by Hiro Matsushita)
- **HQ:** San Clemente, California, United States
- **Type:** Private company, 100% owned by Matsushita International Corp
- **Status:** Active — SULE is an ongoing NASA SBIR-funded flight-test program; Swift's broader business (defense/aerospace contract engineering, tactical UAS, robotics) is separately active and larger in scope
- **Key metrics (SULE):** 72-foot wingspan, under 180 lb weight, up to 15 lb payload capacity; reached 55,904 ft MSL in a 24-hour flight (September 29–30, 2024) and a company-reported altitude record of 67,000 ft MSL in a 24-hour flight (July 26, 2025); designed to target 30+ days aloft at roughly 60,000 feet once mature

## What It Is / How It Works

Swift Engineering was founded in 1983 as Swift Racing Cars, building Formula Ford and other open-wheel race chassis, and was purchased and renamed in 1991 by Hiro Matsushita — a former Indy car driver and grandson of Panasonic founder Kōnosuke Matsushita — who remains Chairman and CEO. Beginning in 1997 the company diversified into aerospace and defense contract engineering, working with Northrop Grumman, Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Sikorsky, and SpaceX on programs spanning UAVs, helicopters, submarines, and composite structures. Its own product lines today include the tactical Swift Crane/Swift-Xi VTOL UAS family (with a Japan-based joint-venture subsidiary, Swift Xi Inc.), Swift Robotics, Swift Global Comm, and the HyperKelp maritime-buoy joint venture, alongside SULE.

SULE is Swift's solar-electric HALE aircraft, designed to climb to stratospheric altitude on battery power and then sustain flight using solar energy, with a long-term goal of 30-day-plus missions at around 60,000 feet for applications including Earth observation, climate monitoring, communications relay, and defense/ISR missions. The program originated from a 2018 NASA proposal and completed its first flight from Spaceport America in New Mexico in July 2020, receiving both a NASA airworthiness certification and an FAA Certificate of Authorization. Development has continued under multiple NASA Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) contracts, including a Phase II award announced in 2025 that funds further design, fabrication, and flight testing toward 24-hour, 48-hour, and eventual 7-day flight demonstrations. The aircraft is built to fit inside a standard 40-foot shipping container and be deployed by a two- to three-person crew, reflecting an "attritable," lower-cost design philosophy rather than a large fixed installation.

Outside of SULE, Swift's press activity through 2025 shows a company more heavily engaged in conventional defense and aerospace manufacturing than in HAPS specifically: it was selected in 2025 to supply a composite fuselage for the Bell Textron-led DARPA SPRINT X-plane program, showcased its Accipiter long-endurance UAS at a Japan defense industry event, and continues legacy work on programs like the Sikorsky-Boeing SB-1 Defiant and Lockheed Martin X-59. This breadth means SULE/HAPS work represents one active technology-demonstration program within a much larger, diversified aerospace engineering and manufacturing business, rather than Swift's core commercial focus.

## Notable Developments

- **July 26, 2025 (reported September–November 2025):** SULE reached an altitude of 67,000 feet MSL during a 24-hour flight from Spaceport America, New Mexico — reported by Swift Engineering and multiple trade outlets as a new company altitude record, surpassing the September 2024 mark.
- **2025 (reported July–September 2025):** Swift Engineering received a Phase II NASA SBIR award (reported as approximately $1.2 million) to continue SULE design, fabrication, and flight-test work toward longer-duration missions.
- **September 29–30, 2024:** SULE reached 55,904 feet MSL during a 24-hour flight at Spaceport America, more than doubling its previous altitude record of 25,000 feet.
- **July 2020:** The Swift HALE UAS (predecessor designation for what became SULE) completed its first flight at Spaceport America in partnership with NASA's Ames Research Center, receiving NASA airworthiness certification and an FAA Certificate of Authorization.
- **2025 (concurrent, non-HAPS):** Swift Engineering was selected as a composite-fuselage supplier for the DARPA SPRINT X-plane program (prime contractor Bell Textron) and showcased its Accipiter long-endurance UAS at Japan's ATLA Industry Day — both illustrating the company's broader defense/UAS business alongside SULE.
