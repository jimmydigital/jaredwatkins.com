## Summary

NewSpace Research & Technologies (NRT) is a Bengaluru, India-based aerospace and defense startup developing a solar-powered High Altitude Pseudo-Satellite (HAPS) intended for stratospheric surveillance missions, alongside a broader portfolio of drones for the Indian military. It holds a Ministry of Defence-backed iDEX (Innovations for Defence Excellence) development contract for a full-scale HAPS aimed at the Indian Navy, with delivery expected around 2027, and has set incremental national endurance records with subscale prototypes en route to that goal.

## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 2018 (one media source states 2019; company materials and most press cite 2018)
- **HQ:** Bengaluru, Karnataka, India
- **Type:** Private company (startup)
- **Status:** Active — HAPS is a subscale, incremental flight-test program; the company also has multiple fielded/contracted non-HAPS drone products (swarm drones, tethered drones, the MAPSS medium-altitude surveillance UAV)
- **Key metrics:** May 2024 flight reached 26,000 feet (7,925 m) for 27 hours, a national endurance record for an indigenous UAV; a December 2023 prototype flight exceeded 21 hours. The planned full-scale HAPS is reported to target a maximum takeoff weight of about 450 kg, wingspan of 24–25 meters, cruise altitude around 65,000 feet (20,000 m), a 35 kg payload, and up to 90 days of stratospheric endurance. Funding: total raised reported at roughly $73–80 million across multiple rounds, including a $21 million Series A (February 2022), $3.21 million pre-Series B (December 2022), and a ₹115 crore (~$13.3 million) debt round (announced August 2025).

## What It Is / How It Works

NewSpace Research & Technologies was founded by Sameer Joshi, a former Indian Air Force fighter pilot, along with co-founders Julius Amrit and Dilip Chabria. The company develops what it calls "persistent drones" for earth observation and communications, and has grown into a broader defense-unmanned-systems supplier to the Indian armed forces, with products spanning swarm loitering munitions (the Sheshnaag family), tethered surveillance drones, and multi-rotor/hybrid platforms (BELUGA, NIMBUS, MACKEREL) in addition to its HAPS program.

NRT's HAPS is a fixed-wing, solar-electric aircraft designed to generate power from onboard solar panels during the day and fly on battery power through the night, intended to operate in the stratosphere — above roughly 65,000 feet — for extended surveillance missions including electro-optical/infrared imaging, synthetic aperture radar, signals intelligence, and communications relay, with the aim of bridging the gap between satellites (infrequent revisit) and conventional long-endurance drones (limited to roughly a day aloft). The program is being developed incrementally: subscale prototypes have progressively extended flight duration and altitude in tests at the Aeronautical Test Range in Chitradurga, Karnataka, ahead of a planned full-scale stratospheric platform. The program is backed by the Ministry of Defence's iDEX initiative, and India's Ministry of Defence disclosed in July 2026 that it had approved acquisition of a fixed-wing HAPS capability without naming the specific developer or contract value; NRT is reported (alongside a separate government-run CSIR-National Aerospace Laboratories program) to be one of two Indian organizations developing an indigenous fixed-wing HAPS, with its version intended for the Indian Navy and delivery expected around 2027.

Separately, in January 2026 the Indian Army placed a ₹168 crore order with NRT for the Medium Altitude Persistent Surveillance System (MAPSS), a solar-electric fixed-wing UAV that ThePrint reported is derived from — but distinct from — the stratospheric HAPS program, adapted instead for medium-altitude, long-endurance border and ISR missions. This reflects NRT's broader pattern of using its HAPS-derived solar-electric airframe and power technology across multiple contracted defense products, not solely toward a single stratospheric aircraft.
