## Summary

Hexcel makes carbon fiber from polyacrylonitrile (PAN) precursor that it produces itself, then converts much of the fiber into prepreg and other composite materials. Aerospace dominates its sales, and two customers (Airbus and Boeing, with their subcontractors) account for roughly 55% of net sales.

## Key Facts

- Type: company; public (Form 10-K filer; ticker HXL)
- Status: Active; capacity expansion in progress
- Carbon fiber sites named in the FY2024 10-K: Salt Lake City, UT (carbon fiber and prepreg; R&T Center of Excellence completed early 2023) and Decatur, AL (new line)
- Brand: HexTow® carbon fibers
- 2024 net sales: Composite Materials $1,531.0M (about 80%) and Engineered Products $372.0M (about 20%); sum about $1,903M (summed here; the 10-K text excerpt did not state a total)
- 2024 sales by market: Commercial Aerospace 63%, Space & Defense 30%, Industrial 7%
- Customer concentration: Airbus and subcontractors about 40%; Boeing and subcontractors about 15%
- Employees: 5,894 full-time and contract workers at 31 Dec 2024, of which 3,120 in the U.S.
- Internal use: 60–65% by value of carbon fiber output used internally in 2023 and 2024; the rest sold to third parties

## What It Is / How It Works

Acrylonitrile is the primary raw material for PAN, and Hexcel states that all PAN it produces is for internal carbon fiber production. Fiber is then used in Hexcel's own composite materials (see [Carbon Fiber Inputs and Precursors]({{< relref "carbon-fiber-inputs-and-precursors.md" >}})). The 10-K excerpt read does not list PAN facility locations.

## Notable Developments

- **Early 2023:** Decatur, AL carbon fiber line construction resumed; expected to be qualified for aerospace production in 2028.
- **Early 2023:** R&T Center of Excellence completed next to the Salt Lake City operations.
