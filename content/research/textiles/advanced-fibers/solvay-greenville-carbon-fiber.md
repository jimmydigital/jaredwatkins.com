## Summary

One of the oldest U.S. carbon fiber plants, the Piedmont (Greenville area) site began in 1965 under Union Carbide and was run by Solvay as of 2017. Its fiber goes mostly into aerospace composites.

## Key Facts

- Type: facility / company unit
- Status: Active as of 2017 article; ownership since then not verified here
- Opened: 1965 (Union Carbide); Textile Excellence says Solvay (via Cytec) produced carbon fibre there since 1981 and expanded in 2016
- Employees: more than 350 (2017)
- Output: yarns of 3,000 or 6,000 filaments on five-pound spools
- Uses: about 90% aerospace; major Boeing supplier; F-35 and other military aircraft; satellites, medical devices, cars
- Precursor route as described: acrylonitrile dissolved in DMSO, forced through platinum-and-gold plates to form filaments, then heated, cooled and stretched
- Capacity: not stated

## What It Is / How It Works

The 2017 article describes wet spinning of an acrylonitrile solution and attributes the textile vocabulary of the process to the Upstate's textile history. Much of the fiber is woven into sheets and combined with proprietary resins (see [Carbon Fiber Inputs and Precursors]({{< relref "carbon-fiber-inputs-and-precursors.md" >}})).
