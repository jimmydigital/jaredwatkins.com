## Summary

The Carbon Fiber Technology Facility (CFTF) is a U.S. Department of Energy-funded, highly instrumented pilot line used to prove out lower-cost precursors and processes with industry partners.

## Key Facts

- Type: government R&D facility (Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Oak Ridge, TN); part of ORNL's Manufacturing Demonstration Facility
- Size: 42,000 sq ft; 390-ft processing line
- Conversion: 25 tonnes/year based on 24k PAN tows; oxidation to 400°C, low-temperature carbonization to 1000°C, high-temperature carbonization to 2000°C
- Melt spinning: 65 tonnes/year of polyolefin precursor fiber, tow or web form
- Precursors: lignin (conversion and melt-spinning), pitch, polyethylene/polyolefin; conversion line baselined for standard-modulus PAN and upgradable to high-modulus PAN and rayon
- In development: microwave and plasma advanced conversion line
- Access: industrial collaborators (emphasis on vertical integration), academia, labs, agencies; export-control compliance required
- Cost goals: not stated on the pages read

## What It Is / How It Works

The facility is the main U.S. route for testing alternatives to expensive PAN, such as lignin and polyolefin (see [Carbon Fiber Inputs and Precursors]({{< relref "carbon-fiber-inputs-and-precursors.md" >}})).
