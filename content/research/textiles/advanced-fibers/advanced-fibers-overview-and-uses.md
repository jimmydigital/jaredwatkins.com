## Summary

High-performance fibers are made from textile-like processes (spinning, drawing, heat treatment) but sell as structural or protective materials. Demand in the sources read comes from aerospace, wind energy, automotive, hydrogen storage, ballistic protection, ropes and jet engines.

## Uses by Market (from sources read)

| Market | Material | Evidence |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Commercial aerospace | PAN carbon fiber | Hexcel: 63% of 2024 sales commercial aerospace; Airbus about 40% and Boeing about 15% of sales ([Hexcel]({{< relref "hexcel.md" >}})) |
| Military aircraft | Carbon fiber | Solvay Greenville: about 90% of output aerospace incl. F-35 ([entry]({{< relref "solvay-greenville-carbon-fiber.md" >}})) |
| Wind blades | Large-tow carbon fiber | Zoltek/Toray: 40k+ filament tow for blades ([entry]({{< relref "zoltek-toray.md" >}})) |

| Body armor, vehicles | UHMWPE, aramid | [Spectra]({{< relref "solstice-advanced-materials-spectra.md" >}}), [Dyneema]({{< relref "avient-dyneema.md" >}}), [Kevlar]({{< relref "dupont-kevlar.md" >}}) |
| Ropes, nets, mooring | UHMWPE | Dyneema applications |
| Jet engines | SiC fiber / CMC | [GE Aerospace]({{< relref "ge-aerospace-sic-fiber.md" >}}) |

## U.S. Producers

Hexcel (Salt Lake City, Decatur) and the Solvay site (Piedmont, SC) are long-standing U.S. makers; Zoltek is U.S.-headquartered but owned by Toray; Teijin built Greenwood (now paused). Global demand context from a 2018 article: about 60,000 metric tons (2015) to about 150,000 (2025 forecast).

## Gaps

Chinese-owned competitors, DowAksa, Mitsubishi Chemical, and current Toray Spartanburg status were not researched here.
