## Summary

Zoltek, based in St. Louis, Missouri, specialised in low-cost "large tow" carbon fiber and was bought by Japan's Toray Industries. Toray later announced expansions of Zoltek's large-tow capacity in Hungary and Mexico, aimed mainly at wind turbine blades.

## Key Facts

- Type: company (Toray subsidiary); Toray is Japanese, listed in Tokyo
- Status: Active
- HQ: St. Louis, MO, USA
- Plants (2013): USA, Hungary, Mexico
- Large tow definition: carbon fiber with 40,000 or more filaments
- 2012 volume: Zoltek supplied almost 10 million lb (4,000 tonnes) of carbon fiber to Vestas
- 2018 announcement: Hungary (Nyergesújfalu) from 10,000 to 15,000 tons/year (investment above $130M; start early 2020); Mexico from 5,000 to 10,000 tons/year; combined about 25,000 tons/year
- Toray in South Carolina: a 2018-era article describes a US$1.4B Toray plant in Spartanburg, SC (to supply Boeing after accreditation); current status not verified here

## What It Is / How It Works

Large-tow fiber trades some per-filament refinement for lower cost per kilogram and is used where volume matters, notably wind blades (larger turbines are expected to increase demand) and increasingly automotive structural parts in Europe. See [Advanced Fibers: Overview and Uses]({{< relref "advanced-fibers-overview-and-uses.md" >}}).

## Notable Developments

- **2013-09-30 (story update):** Toray agreed to buy Zoltek; closing expected late 2013 or early 2014.
- **2018-04-10:** Toray announced the large-tow expansions in Hungary and Mexico.
