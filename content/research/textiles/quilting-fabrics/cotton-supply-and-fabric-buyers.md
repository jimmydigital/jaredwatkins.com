## Summary

Cotton is grown in a handful of countries but milled in a different set. The United States grows about 13.6 million bales (2026/27 USDA projection) but mills only about 1.6 million, exporting roughly 90% as raw fiber to Vietnam, Pakistan, Mexico, Turkey, and others. For finished quilting fabric, the dominant buyer is the United States itself, whose quilting fabric is made mostly overseas.

## Key Facts

- Type: supply-chain / market data
- Status: Projections for marketing year 2026/27 (USDA ERS, August 14, 2026)
- World cotton: production 117.6M bales, mill use 122.9M bales, trade 43.8M bales, stocks-to-use 57%
- U.S.: production 13.6M bales, exports 12.3M, mill use 1.6M, ending stocks 4.0M (1 bale = 480 lb)

Korea, Japan, and Thailand — the other reported quilting-fabric origins — did not appear in the sources fetched.

### Who buys fabric and garments

- **Apparel exports (WTO via Modaes, 2025):** EU 30.8% ($177B), China 27.3% ($157B), Bangladesh $39B, Vietnam 6.5% ($38B), India 3% ($17B), Türkiye 2.9% ($17B), United States 1.1% ($7B). These are exports, which indicate where fabric is converted, not who buys fabric.
- **U.S. textile context (NCTO, 2025):** shipments $60.9B (down from $63.9B), exports $27B, more than 40 plants closed in about 2.5 years; mill employment fell from 503,300 (January 1990) to 78,300 (August 2026) per federal data cited by Forbes.
- **Quilting fabric buyers:** the U.S. has 9–11 million active quilters and an estimated $4.5B annual industry revenue (2025); 64% of supply purchases occur at local quilt shops. Other quilting-fabric countries were not researched.

### Reindustrialization read-through

The raw-fiber surplus is the structural gap: nearly all U.S. cotton leaves before spinning. The domestic recoveries documented so far are downstream and partial — see [Hamrick Mills]({{< relref "hamrick-mills.md" >}}) and [Reshoring Textiles]({{< relref "reshoring-textiles.md" >}}).
