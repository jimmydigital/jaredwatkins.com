## Summary

Quilting-fabric brands are design-and-marketing houses that contract out weaving, dyeing, and printing; they rarely name mills. The only brand-by-brand country-of-manufacture survey found is a 2017 blog investigation, which reported production in Asia for every major brand except one U.S. line. Brand websites checked in October 2026 (Robert Kaufman, Art Gallery) state no country of manufacture.

## Key Facts

- Type: market survey / reference table
- Status: Evidence is mostly 2017; brand sourcing may have changed
- Mills named by any brand: none found
- U.S.-made mainstream line: Clothworks American Made Brand solids
- Japan-integrated: Kokka ("woven, dyed, and finished in Japan")

## What It Is / How It Works

The 2017 survey (Stitched in Color) emailed brands; only Art Gallery replied with detail, and no specific mills were named for most. It concluded that printing and dyeing are "primarily overseas" and highly mechanized, so few workers produce large volumes, and suggested some houses choose mills for technology rather than low labor cost.

### Reading the table

- **Disclosure is the finding.** For the largest U.S. quilting-fabric brands, no mill name is public. Any claim about "best weaver" or "best dyer" among them is therefore unfalsifiable from public data.
- **Domestic options exist but are narrow.** The U.S.-made mainstream solid line and a hand-dye studio are the two quilting-specific domestic sources found; a separate U.S. vertical mill and print chain exists but is not quilting-branded ([Hamrick Mills]({{< relref "hamrick-mills.md" >}})).
- **Treat 2017 as stale.** Tariffs, mill consolidation, and brand changes since then are not captured.
