## Summary

Turning cotton into printed quilting cloth uses five machine families. The documented makers are mostly Swiss, Belgian, Italian, German, and Japanese; the U.S. presence found is in ginning and some dyeing equipment. Most new machines now ship to Asia.

## Key Facts

- Type: technology / supply chain map
- Status: Mature, Asia-driven demand
- 2023 shuttleless-loom shipments: 171 thousand (+52%), 96% to Asia & Oceania (ITMF)
- Weaving-machine market: about USD 6.4B in 2023; Picanol, Toyota, Staubli, Dornier, and Itema together about 30–35% (GM Insights, December 2024)
- Short-staple spindles shipped 2023: 9.78 million, 85% to Asia & Oceania

### Shipment data (ITMF, 2023)

- Shuttleless looms: 171 thousand (+52%). Air-jet 53 thousand (+34%, 97% to Asia & Oceania, China the main investor); water-jet 92 thousand (+96%); rapier/projectile 27 thousand (+0.1%, India the main investor with 10 thousand).
- Short-staple spindles: 9.78 million (+2%); largest investors China, India, Türkiye, Bangladesh, Uzbekistan, Egypt.
- Finishing: stenters 1,833 units (+3%); jigger/beam dyeing 715; air-jet dyeing 772; overflow dyeing 2,122.
- Cotton-relevant note: water-jet looms are synthetic-only, so cotton quilting cloth is woven on air-jet, rapier, or projectile looms; Itema is described as the only maker of projectile looms.

### Printing split

Rotary screen still dominates: Zimmer data show a 97:3 traditional-to-digital mix, SPGPrints said digital was under 10% in 2020. Digital printers (Kornit, Zimmer Colaris) serve short runs.

## Notable Developments

- **2026-06:** Toyota Industries delisted after Toyota Fudosan's tender offer.
- **2024-08:** ITMF published 2023 machinery shipment statistics.
- **2012:** Continental Eagle ended U.S. gin-machinery production at Prattville (announced December 2009).
- **2009-12:** Continental Eagle announced it would move main gin-machinery production from Prattville to India.
