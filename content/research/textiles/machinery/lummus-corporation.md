## Summary

Lummus Corporation builds machinery and replacement parts for cotton ginning, the first mechanical step after harvest. It is headquartered in Savannah, Georgia, and is the only U.S.-based ginning-equipment maker documented in this review as still manufacturing domestically.

## Key Facts

- Type: company (machine builder)
- Status: Active
- HQ: 225 Bourne Boulevard, Savannah, GA 31408
- Value-chain position: Machine builder (ginning)
- Facilities listed: Savannah GA, Lubbock TX, Harlingen TX, Memphis TN, Greenville MS, Cuiabá (Mato Grosso, Brazil), Wilsonton (Queensland, Australia); Savannah Plant 1 and Lubbock assessed as ISO 9001:2015 conformant
- Flagship unit: Lummus Ag Solutions; other lines: oilseed processing (Carver, Gump), synthetic fiber, hemp/kenaf machinery
- Ownership and financials: not disclosed in sources reviewed

## What It Is / How It Works

Lummus supplies equipment in six ginning areas: unloading, drying and precleaning, distribution/feeding/ginning, lint cleaning, condensing and moisture restoration, and pressing and bale handling, plus design, installation, and field service in the U.S. and abroad. Gins separate lint from seed; the lint becomes the bales that U.S. exporters ship to mills (see [Cotton Supply and Fabric Buyers]({{< relref "cotton-supply-and-fabric-buyers.md" >}})).

### History

Company lore dates the firm to 1869, when Connecticut-based Franklin H. Lummus took over a Columbus, Georgia gin works. It incorporated as F. H. Lummus Sons Co. after 1896, shipped its first export order in 1907, introduced the Air Blast Gin in 1909, became Lummus Cotton Gin Co. in 1910, expanded into textile machinery in the 1920s, introduced the All-Metal Cotton Ginning Outfit in 1931, and became Lummus Industries in 1970. After a bankruptcy and ownership turnover it relocated from Columbus to Savannah; local sources conflict on timing (1993 versus 2000). It acquired Carver Oilseed Co. in 2002 and was later renamed Lummus Ag Technology.
