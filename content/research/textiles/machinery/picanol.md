## Summary

Picanol builds high-speed weaving machines at its Ieper headquarters in Belgium. Cotton fabric such as quilting cloth is woven on air-jet, rapier, or projectile machines, and Picanol makes the first two.

## Key Facts

- Type: company (machine builder)
- Status: Active; one of five makers said to hold about 30–35% of the weaving-machine market together with Toyota, Staubli, Dornier, and Itema (GM Insights, December 2024)
- HQ: Steverlyncklaan 15, 8900 Ieper, Belgium
- Founded: 1936 by Charles Steverlynck as Weefautomaten Picañol NV
- Listing: Picanol Group on Euronext Brussels since 1966, ticker PIC (Wikipedia; verify)
- Value-chain position: Machine builder (weaving)
- Products: air-jet (OmniPlus-i Connect, TerryPlus-i Connect, OmniPlus-i TC Connect) and rapier (Ultimax, OptiMax-i Connect, Supermax, GTMax-S/-i 3.0S Connect) looms, parts, harness frames, services
- Sites: plants in Europe and Asia; named: GTP Bandung (Indonesia), GTP São Paulo (Brazil), GTP Mexico (all 2003)
- Sales and headcount: not found in sources reviewed

## What It Is / How It Works

Picanol offers both weft-insertion methods that dominate cotton weaving. Air-jet looms insert weft with compressed air and are fast on fine, light fabrics; rapier looms use mechanical grippers and handle more yarn types. The company's history includes the MDC electronically controlled shuttle machine (1971), OptiMax-i and GT-Max rapiers (2007), and terry-cloth machines (2015). Group activities extend beyond weaving into engineering and other industrial markets. The Picanol site's parent-company link points to Tessenderlo Group's code of conduct, but no ownership relation is stated.

## Notable Developments

- **2015:** TerryMax-i and TERRYplus Summum launched.
- **2007:** OptiMax-i and GT-Max rapier machines launched.
- **2003:** Plants opened in Indonesia, Brazil, and Mexico.
