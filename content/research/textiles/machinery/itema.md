## Summary

Itema makes weaving machines with all three weft-insertion methods — rapier, air-jet, and projectile — and says it is the only manufacturer to do so. Its roots are in Italy's Seriana Valley, with plants in Italy, Switzerland, and China and a U.S. subsidiary.

## Key Facts

- Type: company (machine builder); private (shareholders not named on its site)
- Status: Active; diversifying
- HQ: Colzate, Bergamo province, Italy
- Founder: Gianni Radici (1967)
- Value-chain position: Machine builder (weaving)
- Scale: turnover EUR 241.5 million (year not stated); 894 employees in its statistics block, "more than 1000" in text; 7 manufacturing sites listed; share capital EUR 30.4 million
- Models: R9500EVO, GalileoRX (rapier); A9500EVO, GalileoAX (air-jet); P7300HP V8 (projectile); Hercules, UniRap, Maxiplus
- Subsidiaries listed on Wikipedia: Switzerland, China, India, U.S., Japan

## What It Is / How It Works

Itema grew by absorbing weaving-machine brands including Somet, Sulzer Textil (projectile looms), and Vamatex. It diversified from 2017: a stake in composites supplier Lamiflex, Itematech (technical textiles, 2019), a majority stake in Schoch (reeds and drop wires, 2019), Itemalab (independent from 2021), and a 2023 joint venture, Tofflonit, with Tofflon Science and Technology Group (biopharmaceutical systems). Wikipedia describes it as the only producer of projectile looms, via the former Sulzer line.
