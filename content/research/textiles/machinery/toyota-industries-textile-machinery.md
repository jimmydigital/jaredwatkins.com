## Summary

Toyota Industries began as a maker of automatic looms and still builds shuttleless weaving machines. A trade report (undated, about 2013–2014) said Toyota held the number-one global share in air-jet loom unit sales. Toyota Fudosan acquired all the parent's shares through a tender offer, and Toyota Industries delisted in June 2026.

## Key Facts

- Type: company division (machine builder); parent now wholly owned by Toyota Fudosan
- Status: Active
- HQ: Kariya, Aichi, Japan
- Value-chain position: Machine builder (weaving; spinning)
- Current looms: JAT910 air-jet; LWT810 water-jet (synthetics only)
- Textile-related subsidiaries: Kirloskar Toyota Textile Machinery (India); Uster Technologies (subsidiary since 2012, per the trade report)
- Dated unit data (trade report, ~2013–2014): air-jet loom sales 9,300 units (+101%); net sales ¥73.1 billion (+83%); JAT810 (production from August 2013) cut air use about 20% versus JAT710

## What It Is / How It Works

Toyota's air-jet looms insert weft with air and suit cotton; its water-jet looms work only with synthetic fibers, so they are irrelevant to cotton quilting cloth. Growth drivers cited were China, India, and ASEAN. The trade report names no air-jet production site. Spinning machinery is mentioned historically (automated continuous spinning system, 1964), and a TCO12 comber was co-developed with Trützschler.

## Notable Developments

- **2026-06:** Delisted after Toyota Fudosan's tender offer.
- **2013-08:** JAT810 production started.
- **2012:** Uster Technologies became a subsidiary.
