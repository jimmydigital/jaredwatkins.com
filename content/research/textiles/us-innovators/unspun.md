## Summary

Unspun builds a 3D weaving machine that produces semi-finished woven garments directly from yarn, replacing many cut-and-sew steps. It is an apparel-weaving innovator, not a flat-fabric or quilting-cloth maker, but it is the most prominent U.S. startup reinventing the loom-to-product step.

## Key Facts

- Type: company
- Status: Emerging; moving from technology development to deployment
- HQ: California (city not stated in sources); headquarters is a microfactory
- Value-chain position: Machine developer / manufacturer (weaving and garment forming)
- Funding: $14M Series A (2023); $32M Series B (July 2024, led by DCVC; Lowercarbon Capital, E12, SOSV, Decathlon revenue-based financing); more than $50M venture capital total (April 2026)
- Leadership: Arne Arens, CEO (appointed March 2026; formerly Global Brand President of The North Face); Beth Esponnette, co-founder
- Brand backers: Walmart and REI letters of support (April 2026)

## What It Is / How It Works

The machine (named Vega in 2024 coverage; the company site now names an "OneWeave" system) weaves a garment from yarn, which the company says takes about 10 minutes for a pair of pants. The model targets low-inventory, nearshore, automated supply chains. In April 2026 the company said it has equipment ready and is evaluating sites in multiple states, with supply-chain partners Bethel Industries, Peckham, and PDS Ltd / GSC Link.
