## Summary

Textile innovation with U.S. roots clusters in three areas: recycling and bio-based inputs, dyeing and printing, and regional manufacturing programs. A recurring pattern is that U.S.-founded technology is built or scaled offshore — Circ's first commercial plant is in France, and ColorZen's 2012 facility was in China.

## Key Facts

- Type: landscape overview
- Status: Mixed; most items are early-stage or unconfirmed
- Regional programs: NSF Textile Innovation Engine of North Carolina; FutureTEX (NC State-led defense textile initiative)

## What It Is / How It Works

### Recycling and inputs

- **Circ** (Danville, Virginia HQ) uses a patented hydrothermal process to separate and recover polyester and cotton from polycotton. Its planned commercial plant is in Saint-Avold, France: about 70,000 metric tons per year, roughly €450M, with construction scheduled for late 2026 pending permits and full operation targeted for 2028 (announced May 19, 2025). Partners are Worley, GEA, and Andritz.
- **Ambercycle** (Los Angeles, founded 2015) makes regenerated polyester under the cycora brand.
- **Selenis** (Fayetteville, NC; part of IMG Group) was partnered with Sweden's Syre on a textile-to-textile polyester plant in Cedar Creek, NC, at up to 10,000 metric tons per year, targeted for mid-2025; its current status was not confirmed.

### Dyeing and printing

- **ColorZen** (New York HQ) pretreats cotton so it takes dye more readily; 2012 company claims were about 90% less water, 75% less energy, and one-third of the time, with manufacturing in China. Current status was not confirmed.
- **Colorifix** (Norwich, UK) uses synthetic biology to produce and fix dyes; $43.7M raised (CB Insights). It is non-U.S.
- **Spoonflower** (Durham, NC) prints cotton by digital inkjet on large-format printers modified to run fabric. Founded 2008, acquired by Shutterfly in 2021; its Durham plant closed in 2023, with production at Shutterfly facilities in Fort Mill, SC, and Tempe, AZ, as of August 2023. Later status was not confirmed.

### Regional programs

- **NSF Textile Innovation Engine of North Carolina** (CEO Anne Wiper): $15M initial NSF investment, $16M renewal that could extend to $45M over three years, more than 500 partners, and $70M of additional funding attracted. Focus areas: textile hemp, recycling, automated manufacturing, biomedical textiles, education.
- **FutureTEX** (NC State-led defense textile initiative): $36M in first-year funding with a ceiling of $480M over ten years.
