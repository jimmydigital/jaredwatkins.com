## Summary

Kinder Morgan, Inc. (NYSE: KMI) is a Houston-based energy infrastructure company that Wikipedia describes as the largest U.S. natural gas pipeline operator (about 65,000 miles of gas pipe and a claimed share of roughly 40% of U.S. gas consumption; a secondary-source figure as of 2024). **"KMP" refers to Kinder Morgan Energy Partners, L.P.**, the master limited partnership that was folded into KMI when the roll-up of KMP, Kinder Morgan Management and El Paso Pipeline Partners closed on 2014-11-26; KMP no longer trades separately ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinder_Morgan)). KMI is a **pipeline operator and shipper-contracted transporter**: it does not own data center power plants, and in the sources reviewed none of its sanctioned projects is a behind-the-meter (BTM) arrangement. Its data center relevance is indirect but large: it builds interstate and intrastate capacity that feeds gas-fired generation (grid-connected and potentially on-site) and local utilities. In Q2 2026 KMI reported a $9.6B sanctioned project backlog, about 92% natural gas and more than 60% supporting power generation and local distribution demand, and said it is developing projects for more than 10 Bcf/d of potential gas-fired power demand ([PGJ](https://pgjonline.com/news/2026/july/kinder-morgan-raises-2026-outlook-as-gas-project-backlog-tops-96-billion), [RBN](https://rbnenergy.com/daily-posts/analyst-insight/q2-2026-earnings-calls-kinder-morgans-natural-gas-project-backlog-keeps)). The share of that attributable to data centers is not quantified in any source reviewed.

## What It Is / How It Works

KMI earns fee-based revenue from shippers who sign long-term firm transportation (FT) agreements; new capacity is typically sanctioned after precedent agreements are signed. For data center power, the relevant products are (1) large interstate expansions into the Southeast and Gulf Coast power corridors, (2) laterals to individual gas plants (for example the Cumberland lateral to a TVA plant), and (3) intrastate and Texas-area capacity. A BTM data center campus would buy gas through a lateral off one of these systems; none of the projects below is described by KMI as BTM.

## Notable Developments

- **2026-10 (about the first week):** The Southern Environmental Law Center, representing nonprofits and landowners, sued over FERC's approval of MSX and SSE4, arguing FERC acted unlawfully by finding need from contracts that may not be arm's length (many shippers are alleged Kinder Morgan affiliates). A London Economics International report commissioned for the suit estimates the pipelines would carry over four times more gas than utilities' public forecasts, attributing much of the gap to speculative future data centers. Kinder Morgan called the suit meritless. No ruling reported. ([GPB, 2026-10-06, updated 10-09](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/10/06/500-miles-of-pipeline-serve-data-center-demand-faces-legal-battle))
- **2026-07-31:** FERC issued certificates for MSX and SSE4 (about $5.2B combined, more than 3.8 MMDth/d). FERC denied the Sierra Club and others an evidentiary hearing and found no evidence of self-dealing that would justify looking behind signed contracts. Each project has three years from the order to be in service. ([PGJ](https://pgjonline.com/news/2026/august/ferc-approves-5-b-kinder-morgan-pipeline-expansion-to-boost-southeast-gas-supply))
- **2026-07 (Q2 call):** Backlog $9.6B; Gulf Coast Express expansion and Cumberland placed in service; management acknowledged that turbine and compression equipment demand is pressuring construction timelines; Permian Link and Station 219 South identified as the next wave. ([RBN](https://rbnenergy.com/daily-posts/analyst-insight/q2-2026-earnings-calls-kinder-morgans-natural-gas-project-backlog-keeps))

## Supply Chain Position

Pipeline operator downstream of producers (Permian, Haynesville, Marcellus/Utica supply) and upstream of utilities, merchant generators and, in principle, data center campuses. Constraints named by management: turbine and compression equipment lead times. Construction is subcontracted; see [MasTec]({{< relref "mastec.md" >}}) and [Quanta]({{< relref "quanta-services.md" >}}) for the builder layer.
