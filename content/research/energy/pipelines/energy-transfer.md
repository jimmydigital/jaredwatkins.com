## Summary

Energy Transfer LP (NYSE: ET) is a diversified midstream partnership whose pipelines include large Texas intrastate systems and interstate lines such as Transwestern. Headquartered in Dallas, Texas per its FY2025 10-K cover ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1276187/000127618726000013/et-20251231.htm)). Of the pipeline operators reviewed, it discloses the most data-center-specific contracting, including at least one **behind-the-meter** campus. All figures in this entry come from ET's own Q1 2026 earnings presentation (2026-05-05) unless noted; they are company claims and mix binding and conditional agreements, which are separated below.

## Key Facts

- **Status:** active
- **Type:** Public master limited partnership (NYSE: ET); value-chain position: **pipeline operator** (intrastate and interstate), with small on-site generation
- **2026 guidance (May 2026):** adjusted EBITDA $18.2B–$18.6B (raised from $17.45B–$17.85B); growth capital about $5.5B–$5.9B
- **Contracted capacity (company):** 6+ Bcf/d of contracted pipeline capacity, 18-year weighted average life, $25B+ expected firm-transport fee revenue (spans all customers, not only data centers)

### Infrastructure

- **Hugh Brinson pipeline:** about 400 miles, Waha to ET's network south of Dallas–Fort Worth; Phase I about 1.5 Bcf/d (42-inch) sold out, in service Q4 2026 expected; Phase II compression to about 2.2 Bcf/d west–east expected Q1 2027; about $2.7B combined.
- **Desert Southwest Expansion (Transwestern):** about 520 miles, 48-inch, up to about 2.3 Bcf/d from the Permian to New Mexico and Arizona; up to about $5.6B excluding AFUDC; FERC pre-filing March 2026, certificate filing expected Q4 2026, in service by Q4 2029.
- **Springerville Lateral:** about 120 miles, 30-inch, about 625 MMcf/d to gas generation replacing two coal plants; about $600M; Q4 2029.
- **Own generation:** constructing eight 10 MW gas-fired facilities in Texas, two online.

## What It Is / How It Works

ET sells firm transportation and, in some cases, supply, to data center developers and utilities. Because the Texas lines are intrastate they sit under state rather than FERC jurisdiction (general regulatory context), which is one reason ET can announce Texas campus connections faster than interstate competitors. The Nexus arrangement is the clearest BTM pattern: the pipeline delivers fuel to an on-site generation plant that is not connected as a grid-supplied load.
