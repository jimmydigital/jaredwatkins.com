## Summary

Baker Hughes supplies gas turbines and compression equipment, the **compressor-station layer** that management at pipeline operators cited as a lead-time constraint. Per Turbomachinery Magazine's summary of its Q2 2026 results, Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) orders were $7.1B (book-to-bill 2.2) with remaining performance obligations at an all-time high of $37.1B; Gas Technology Equipment orders were $4.913B, up more than fivefold. Most named compression awards are LNG-related (Venture Global, Cheniere/Bechtel's Sabine Pass Train 7, Golar) or Middle East. Power and data center awards include **76 NovaLT16 gas turbines (about 1.3 GW) for Dynamis Power Solutions** and **an initial 1 GW award from Kodiak Gas Services with a path to 1.8 GW** ([Turbomachinery Magazine](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/baker-hughes-iet-backlog-hits-record-37-1b-on-lng-and-power-generation-surge)). The article names no pipeline compressor-station order and no Baker Hughes executives.

## What It Is / How It Works

Gas turbines and compressors drive pipeline compressor stations and, in smaller units, on-site power for data centers and oilfield loads. Order growth there reads across to behind-the-meter generation (see [Behind-the-Meter Power]({{< relref "../../../datacenters/behind-meter-power/_index.md" >}})) and to pipeline expansions that need added compression.
