## Summary

MasTec, Inc. (NYSE: MTZ) is a **pipeline builder / infrastructure contractor** (not an owner of pipelines or generation). It is the clearest public read-through on gas pipeline construction demand. In Q2 2026 its Pipeline Infrastructure segment had revenue of $643M and EBITDA of about $119M (18.4% margin), with about $1.8B backlog, up 35% sequentially at 1.7x book-to-bill; management expects second-half 2027 pipeline activity to be substantially larger than the first half and is optimistic about 2028-2029 ([Nasdaq call summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/mastec-q2-earnings-call-highlights)). The source does not say how much of the work is data-center-driven.

## Key Facts

- **Status:** active
- **Type:** Public (NYSE: MTZ); value-chain position: **pipeline builder / EPC-type contractor**
- **Q2 2026:** revenue $4.374B (+23%); adjusted EBITDA $384M (+40%); backlog record $21.4B
- **2026 guidance (raised):** revenue $18.2B; adjusted EBITDA $1.6B; adjusted EPS $9.30
- **Clean Energy and Infrastructure:** backlog about $7.8B; pursuing simple-cycle, reciprocating-engine and turnkey data center work
- **Superior Group:** acquisition closed July 2026; adds electrical capability for data centers, about 3,000 employees
- **Hyperscaler pursuits:** multiple projects over $1B, some affecting 2027

## What It Is / How It Works

MasTec builds large-diameter pipelines under contracts with operators such as those in this section; it also builds power and electrical infrastructure. Backlog is a capacity indicator for operators' sanctioned projects (for example MSX, SSE4, Hugh Brinson), but contractor names on individual projects were not verified here.

## Materials & Supply Chain

MasTec is a contractor, not a manufacturer. Per its FY2025 10-K ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/15615/000001561526000020/mtz-20251231.htm)), on many contracts customers furnish the materials and MasTec installs them; on others MasTec buys materials on the customer's behalf from third parties; the company says it is not dependent on any one vendor, and substantially all equipment comes from third-party vendors.
