## Summary

Sulfur is a by-product of oil and gas refining and gas processing, produced in every major hydrocarbon region. It is the cathode material in lithium-sulfur (Li-S) cells such as [Lyten]({{< relref "../batteries/lyten.md" >}})'s, and the feedstock for lithium sulfide (Li₂S), the precursor to sulfide solid electrolytes used by Solid Power, QuantumScape and Idemitsu/Toyota. Sulfur is no longer a negligible-cost input: contract prices roughly tripled during 2025 and, according to S&P Global, Indonesian nickel HPAL producers were paying $1,100–$1,200 per tonne by mid-2026 after disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz and export restrictions elsewhere. Direct battery use is tiny against fertilizer and metals leaching, so the main exposure for this research area is indirect, through nickel and cobalt costs.

## Key Facts

- **Chemical symbol:** S (atomic number 16)
- **Primary industrial uses:** sulfuric acid for phosphate fertilizer (largest use), metals leaching (including nickel HPAL), rubber, chemicals; battery materials are a small share
- **World production:** about 83.9 million t (2024) and 84.0 million t (2025e) ([USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-sulfur.pdf))
- **Largest producers (2025e, million t):** China 19.0, US 8.1, Russia 7.5, Saudi Arabia 7.2, UAE 6.3, Kazakhstan 4.8, Canada 5.0, India 3.7 ([USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-sulfur.pdf)); Gulf producers (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Iran) together are roughly 20 million t
- **Price, US average unit value:** $180/t in 2025 versus $46.42/t in 2024; Tampa contract price began 2025 at $116 per long ton and reached $310 in early October 2025 ([USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-sulfur.pdf))
- **Price, imported (Indonesia):** $182/t to $483/t during 2025, up 165% ([Cobalt Institute](https://www.cobaltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Cobalt-Market-Report-2025.pdf)); granular sulfur CFR Indonesia $1,050/t on 9 July 2026 ([S&P Global via Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1140965))
- **Market outlook:** CRU describes the sulphur market as entering a deficit expected to last until 2028 (summary only; full article paywalled) ([CRU](https://www.bcinsight.crugroup.com/2026/01/28/the-sulphur-market-in-deficit/))
- **Geographic concentration:** production is widely distributed, but seaborne trade is concentrated; S&P Global reports about half of traded sulfur (about 20 million t per year) passes through the Strait of Hormuz ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/071426-sulfur-supply-shocks-reshape-nickel-costs-to-help-gcc-producers-overcome-gulf-reliance))

## Battery-Specific Uses

### 1. Lithium-Sulfur (Li-S) Battery Cathode

In Li-S cells, sulfur is the cathode active material and reacts with lithium on discharge to form lithium sulfide compounds. The long-standing obstacle is the "polysulfide shuttle": dissolved intermediates migrate to the anode and degrade the cell. [Lyten]({{< relref "../batteries/lyten.md" >}}) uses a 3D graphene scaffold to trap sulfur. Li-S needs no nickel, cobalt or graphite, and the sulfur volume per cell is small, so Lyten's direct exposure to the sulfur price spike is limited.

### 2. Sulfide Solid Electrolyte Precursor (Li₂S)

Lithium sulfide is the key precursor from which sulfide solid electrolytes are synthesized, including argyrodite-type materials. Converting elemental sulfur into high-purity battery-grade Li₂S is the harder step: the compound is moisture-sensitive and needs controlled manufacturing. Idemitsu Kosan is building a lithium sulfide plant within its Chiba Complex for about ¥21.3 billion, with a ¥7.1 billion grant from Japan's METI, producing about 1,000 t per year (equivalent to about 3 GWh of batteries per year) from June 2027 ([Electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2025/03/01/idemitsu-kosan-builds-plant-for-lithium-sulphide/), [Battery-News](https://battery-news.de/en/2025/03/05/idemitsu-to-build-lithium-sulfide-plant-for-solid-state-batteries/)). It is the only large dedicated Li₂S investment documented in this knowledge base, and Toyota is the first intended customer.

## Idemitsu's Structural Position

[Idemitsu Kosan]({{< relref "../batteries/idemitsu-solid-state.md" >}}) is an oil company with its own refining by-product sulfur and a battery materials business that converts sulfur into Li₂S and solid electrolyte. This integration from refining to electrolyte is not matched by any other documented company. Solid Power and QuantumScape both use sulfide-based electrolytes and have not disclosed their Li₂S sources.

**⚑ Shared supplier risk:** as sulfide solid-state programs scale, Li₂S supply could become a bottleneck. Idemitsu's plant is sized at roughly 3 GWh per year and Toyota is its first intended customer, so capacity for other developers is not established.

## Geopolitical Risk Assessment

Sulfur production is geographically diverse, but seaborne supply is concentrated in the Gulf and Russia/Kazakhstan, and recent trade restrictions showed how quickly it tightens. HPAL nickel is the most exposed major consumer, at roughly 10 tonnes of sulfur per tonne of nickel, so each $100 per tonne of sulfur adds about $1,000 per tonne to nickel cost ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/071426-sulfur-supply-shocks-reshape-nickel-costs-to-help-gcc-producers-overcome-gulf-reliance)). That ties sulfur to the [Nickel]({{< relref "nickel.md" >}}) and [Cobalt]({{< relref "cobalt.md" >}}) entries.

For solid-state and Li-S developers the direct exposure is small. The more specific risk is at the **Li₂S conversion step**, where Idemitsu is the primary documented manufacturer.
