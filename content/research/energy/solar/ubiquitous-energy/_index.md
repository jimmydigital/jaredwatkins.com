## Summary

Ubiquitous Energy was a Redwood City, California company that developed transparent solar technology to turn windows and other transparent surfaces into power generators. It raised more than $70M over 13 years, including a $30M Series B announced in January 2022 from backers including Andersen Corp., Red Cedar Ventures and Riverhorse Investments. Canary Media's December 2024 review of failed cleantech companies reports that it stopped operating in April and sent employees home; the article does not state the year, which is inferred to be 2024 from its date.

## Key Facts

- **HQ:** Redwood City, California
- **Founded:** 2011 per a January 2022 report, by researchers from MIT and Michigan State University
- **Status:** ceased operations (April, reported December 2024)
- **Funding:** more than $70M in total; $30M Series B (January 2022)
- **Partners (2022):** global partners including ENEOS Holdings
- **CEO (2022):** Susan Stone, who said the Series B moved the company closer to mass production by 2024
- **Stated goal (2022):** one billion square feet of transparent solar windows installed by 2050

## What It Is / How It Works

The company's technology added solar generation to transparent surfaces such as windows. Canary Media's explanation of the failure is market-side: persuading electricians, architects and builders to adopt building-integrated solar proved hard.
