## Summary

Swift Solar is a US perovskite solar company founded in 2017 as a spin-out from Stanford, MIT and NREL. On 2026-03-12 it announced the acquisition of core manufacturing assets, IP and team from Meyer Burger, the European maker that filed for bankruptcy in 2025, to build a gigawatt-scale heterojunction (HJT) cell and module factory in the United States and later add perovskite tandems to it. The acquisition price and the factory site were not disclosed.

## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 2017 (spin-out of Stanford, MIT and NREL)
- **HQ:** not confirmed in the sources reviewed
- **Type:** Private
- **Funding:** $27M Series A in June 2024 co-led by Eni Next and Fontinalis Partners, which Mercom reports brought total funding to $44M; EnergyTrend says over $60M raised in total. The two figures differ
- **Acquisition (announced 2026-03-12):** Meyer Burger GW-scale HJT manufacturing equipment, global IP portfolio and a team of manufacturing and silicon specialists; price undisclosed
- **Plan:** within 1–2 years a GW-scale HJT cell and module factory in the US; within 3–5 years a perovskite layer added to the line, targeting cell efficiency above 30%
- **Lab result cited:** 30.99% HJT-perovskite tandem cell efficiency (EnergyTrend, company-sourced)

## What It Is / How It Works

Swift Solar develops perovskite thin-film cells and perovskite-on-silicon tandems. The Meyer Burger purchase gives it a mature silicon heterojunction production line on which a perovskite top layer can later be added, so it can start by selling HJT silicon product and move to tandems.

## Supply Chain Position

Swift Solar is moving from a perovskite developer toward a silicon-based manufacturer: HJT cell and module production first, tandem later. This puts it in the US high-efficiency silicon segment where Meyer Burger's Arizona operations had failed.
