## Summary

NuCube Energy is a microreactor startup co-founded in 2023 at Idealab Studio by serial entrepreneur Bill Gross and nuclear engineer Dr. Cristian Rabiti (CEO), developing a high-temperature, TRISO-fueled, heat-pipe-cooled microreactor pitched as having no moving parts and delivering industrial heat up to 1,100°C alongside electricity. The company is the smallest and least funded of DOE's first four Nuclear Energy Launch Pad selections, having raised $13 million in a February 2026 round led by Arizona Nuclear Ventures. Unlike Deployable Energy and Radiant Industries, which test at Idaho National Laboratory under the Launch Pad INL pathway, NuCube was selected under the Launch Pad USA pathway together with Idaho State University to deploy its Advanced Research and Test (ART) Reactor on ISU's Pocatello, Idaho campus, where it will serve as both an operator-training platform and a commercialization testbed. NuCube is separately validating autonomous operation and remote monitoring with Argonne National Laboratory through a DOE GAIN voucher, and opened a Phoenix, Arizona office in May 2026 to pursue the state's data center and industrial power demand growth.

## What It Is / How It Works

**Heat-pipe cooling, no moving parts:** NuCube's central engineering claim is a solid-state, heat-pipe-cooled reactor design with no pumps or moving mechanical parts in the primary heat transport path — heat pipes passively conduct thermal energy from the reactor core outward using a sealed working-fluid phase-change cycle, a design approach shared conceptually (though not identically implemented) with other heat-pipe microreactor concepts pursued elsewhere in the advanced reactor industry. The company frames "simplicity" as the core lever for both affordability and safety, arguing that eliminating moving parts and active cooling systems reduces both capital cost and the number of failure modes requiring active mitigation.

**TRISO fuel, high-temperature output:** Like Radiant Industries and the DOE Launch Pad cohort generally, NuCube uses TRISO particle fuel. The claimed 1,100°C output temperature is well above what light water reactors can deliver and is aimed at industrial process heat customers (chemical processing, hydrogen production, desalination) in addition to electricity generation — a dual heat-and-power pitch distinct from the pure-electricity focus of Deployable Energy's Unity or Radiant's Kaleidos.

**Launch Pad USA vs. Launch Pad INL:** DOE's Nuclear Energy Launch Pad offers two pathways: Launch Pad INL, for projects tested at Idaho National Laboratory (the pathway used by Deployable Energy and Radiant Industries), and Launch Pad USA, for projects deployed outside INL with DOE regulatory and technical support extended to the chosen site. NuCube and Idaho State University's ART Reactor deployment on ISU's Pocatello campus is a Launch Pad USA project — the reactor will sit at a university, not a national laboratory, functioning simultaneously as a commercial technology demonstration and an academic/operator-training resource. This is a notably different institutional model than the other three Launch Pad companies in this section, which all test at INL.

**Idealab Studio origin:** NuCube was co-founded within Idealab Studio, the incubator arm of Bill Gross's Idealab — a decades-old Pasadena startup studio with an established (if mixed) record of hardware and energy ventures. This gives NuCube a different institutional lineage than the venture-capital-backed, aerospace-alumni-founded pattern seen at Deployable Energy and Radiant Industries elsewhere in this section.
