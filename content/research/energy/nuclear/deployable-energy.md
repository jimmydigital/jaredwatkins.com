## Summary

Deployable Energy is a Houston, Texas microreactor startup founded in 2025 by CEO Bobby Gallagher, building the Unity Nuclear Battery (UNB) — a 1 MWe gas-cooled microreactor sized to fit a standard shipping container and designed for factory manufacture rather than site-built construction. Unity reached criticality at Idaho National Laboratory on June 30, 2026, about 150 days after project kickoff, making Deployable the third company to hit the Trump administration's July 4, 2026 deadline for three US reactors to achieve criticality under DOE's Reactor Pilot Program / Nuclear Energy Launch Pad. The company is self-funded and has not disclosed a priced institutional round, but in August 2026 alone it announced strategic equity investments from Solaris Energy Infrastructure (NYSE: SEI) and Hornbeck Offshore, on top of an earlier $22.5 billion, 40-year data center power pipeline deal with GridMarket. Unity is aimed at remote, defense, maritime, and behind-the-meter industrial/data center power rather than grid-scale generation; commercial deployment is targeted for 2028.

## What It Is / How It Works

**"Nuclear as a manufactured product":** Deployable Energy's founding thesis, echoed across its materials, is that nuclear power should be built like an industrial product on a factory line rather than engineered as a bespoke construction project each time. The company explicitly avoids "large forgings, exotic materials, exotic coatings" that lengthen lead times for other advanced reactor designs, instead using conventional low-enriched uranium fuel and materials sourced from existing industrial supply chains. This positions Unity as a low-HALEU-risk design relative to sodium-fast or pebble-bed competitors (Oklo, X-energy, TerraPower) that depend on a thin domestic HALEU enrichment base.

**Gas-cooled, container-scale microreactor:** Unity is a 1 MWe gas-cooled reactor using a helium primary loop and standard LEU uranium dioxide fuel, engineered to be transportable within a standard 20-foot shipping container footprint. The pitch is a "nuclear battery" — a sealed, factory-built unit that can be shipped to a site, operated for years without refueling, and removed or replaced rather than maintained in place like a conventional plant. This form factor targets use cases where grid power is unavailable, unreliable, or operationally undesirable: remote communities, defense installations, humanitarian/disaster response, offshore and maritime vessels, and behind-the-meter industrial or data center loads.

**Houston as a manufacturing base:** Gallagher has been explicit that Houston was chosen deliberately to draw on the region's oil-and-gas-honed capacity for manufacturing industrial equipment, pressure vessels, and vessels/marine hardware at scale — the same logic several other nuclear and energy startups in this research section apply to shipyard or offshore-fabrication-based construction (compare [Blue Energy]({{< relref "blue-energy.md" >}})'s shipyard-built LWR approach).

**DOE Reactor Pilot Program pathway:** Unity's criticality test proceeded under DOE authorization at a national laboratory site (INL/NRIC) rather than through the NRC's civilian reactor licensing process, which is a separate and much longer pathway. Deployable opened NRC pre-application activities (Project 99902155) in October 2025 for the follow-on step of siting, construction, and operation of commercial units — this is preliminary engagement, not a Construction Permit or Combined License application, and should not be read as NRC authorization to build or operate a commercial reactor.

**Maritime and offshore push:** The August 2026 Hornbeck Offshore MOU is Deployable's most concrete articulation of a specific market: inland waterway vessels, offshore platforms and vessels, national security/autonomous vessel logistics, power barges, and floating data centers. The stated commercial target is at least a 20% total-cost-of-ownership reduction versus marine diesel or grid power over a 10-year horizon, at multi-gigawatt scale — a claim that, like Unity's other commercial targets, is unproven at this stage (see Claim Verification).

## Claim Verification

### Claim: Unity reached criticality in ~150 days from project kickoff
**Status:** Partially verified — the ~150-day figure and the June 30, 2026 criticality date are consistently reported, but the DOE PDSA approval date that anchors the "kickoff" baseline is inconsistently reported (May 29 vs. June 23, 2026) across otherwise credible trade outlets

**Supporting:** DOE Secretary Chris Wright, INL Director John Wagner, and CEO Bobby Gallagher are all quoted with the ~150-day figure in the official July 1, 2026 press release; the University of Texas McCombs coverage independently reports "155 days," a close but not identical figure, suggesting the number is a rounded approximation rather than a precisely tracked figure across all sources

**Refuting / questioning:** NEI Magazine dates DOE's Preliminary Documented Safety Analysis approval to May 29, 2026 ("106 days from program kickoff"), while NucNet dates a DOE documented-safety-analysis approval to June 23, 2026 — these may describe two different safety-review milestones rather than a genuine contradiction, but the public reporting does not clearly distinguish them, and neither this entry nor the underlying sources can currently resolve which date anchors the "150 days" claim

**Summary:** The criticality achievement itself (June 30, 2026, at NRIC/INL) is well corroborated across the company's own release, DOE, and multiple trade press. The precise 150-day timeline should be read as an approximate, company-promoted figure rather than a precisely audited one.

### Claim: $22.5 billion GridMarket pipeline / 3+ GW by 2035
**Status:** Unverified as binding commercial revenue

**Supporting:** The deal was announced via GlobeNewswire (a wire service, per this section's sourcing preference for funding/commercial facts) on July 7, 2026, with specific figures (3+ GW target, 500 MW/year 2030–2035 ramp, 40-year term) attributed to both companies

**Refuting / questioning:** No operating or under-construction Unity unit exists yet outside the single INL demonstration reactor; GridMarket is described in coverage as an energy-project "facilitator" rather than a hyperscaler or industrial end customer itself, meaning the $22.5B figure represents an intermediary's projected pipeline rather than signed end-customer contracts; Deployable's own commercial production targets (2028 commercial units, GW-scale by 2030, 10,000 units/year by the mid-2030s) require the company to scale from zero commercial units to mass manufacturing in roughly two years, a timeline with no precedent among the companies tracked in this research section

**Summary:** The GridMarket announcement is real and well-sourced as an announced partnership, but the $22.5B figure should be read as a target pipeline value, not contracted or binding revenue. Track for conversion to named end-customer PPAs.

### Claim: Unity avoids HALEU/exotic-material supply constraints by design
**Status:** Partially verified

**Supporting:** NRC pre-application materials independently confirm Unity uses "standard low enriched uranium dioxide fuel" rather than HALEU, consistent with company statements; this is a verifiable design choice, not just marketing language

**Refuting / questioning:** Using standard LEU does reduce fuel-supply risk relative to HALEU-dependent designs (Oklo, X-energy, Kairos), but it does not eliminate execution risk elsewhere — DOE-scale testing has not yet demonstrated the company's factory-manufacturing and supply-chain claims at the volumes implied by its 2028–2035 targets

**Summary:** The LEU fuel choice is a genuine, verifiable risk reduction relative to HALEU-dependent Gen IV competitors in this section. The broader "manufactured product" cost and speed claims remain unproven at commercial scale.
