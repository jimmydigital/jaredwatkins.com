## Summary

General Matter is a Los Angeles uranium enrichment startup founded by CEO Scott Nolan, a former SpaceX propulsion engineer and Founders Fund partner, and COO Lee Robinson. The company was incubated inside Founders Fund for roughly a year before emerging from stealth in April 2025 with a $50 million Series A led by Founders Fund and Peter Thiel joining the board. Unlike the reactor developers elsewhere in this section, General Matter sits at the fuel-supply layer: it aims to build a domestic enrichment capability spanning LEU, LEU+, and HALEU (5–20% U-235), directly targeting the gap left by Centrus Energy's position as the only currently NRC-licensed US HALEU enricher. In January 2026 the company won a $900 million, 10-year DOE IDIQ contract to build enrichment capacity at the former Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant in Kentucky (~$1.5 billion total investment, ~140 jobs), and in February 2026 signed a separate DOE lease to explore reactivating the long-dormant Fuels and Materials Examination Facility (FMEF) at the Hanford Site in Washington. General Matter was one of DOE's first four Nuclear Energy Launch Pad selections in April 2026 — the only fuel-focused company among Deployable Energy, NuCube Energy, and Radiant Industries.

## What It Is / How It Works

**Filling a domestic HALEU gap:** General Matter's founding thesis, per CEO Scott Nolan, is that no credible American commercial enrichment company existed to fill the emerging HALEU demand from advanced reactor developers — a gap he searched for an investment target to fill during his time at Founders Fund before deciding to found the company instead. This directly addresses the single most-cited supply chain risk across this research section's nuclear entries: Centrus Energy is currently the only NRC-licensed US HALEU enricher, and Russia's TENEX/Rosatom (the prior primary Western HALEU source) is now sanctioned. General Matter's stated aim is to become a second domestic entrant across the full LEU-to-HALEU enrichment range, not just HALEU alone.

**Undisclosed enrichment technology:** General Matter has not publicly disclosed the specific enrichment technology or process it uses (centrifuge cascade design being the presumed but unconfirmed approach, consistent with all current US and allied enrichment capacity). Information submitted to the NRC is treated as proprietary. This opacity makes independent technical evaluation of the company's claims difficult — it is not yet possible to assess technology readiness level from public sources.

**Two-site strategy:** General Matter is pursuing capacity at two distinct DOE legacy nuclear sites simultaneously. Paducah is the primary, funded buildout (backed by the $900M DOE IDIQ) and is a former uranium enrichment site itself (gaseous diffusion, decommissioned), giving it existing site infrastructure, characterization data, and community familiarity with enrichment operations. Hanford's FMEF lease is comparatively exploratory — a site-characterization and feasibility phase for a facility that has never operated, originally built for an entirely different mission (liquid fast breeder reactor fuel/materials support) that was cancelled before startup.

**Regulatory pathway distinct from reactor developers:** Unlike the reactor companies elsewhere in this section, General Matter's core regulatory hurdle is an NRC Part 70 special nuclear material license for enrichment, handling, storage, and transportation of HALEU — a different licensing track than the Construction Permit/Combined License process reactor developers pursue. As of the company's December 2024 Letter of Intent, this process was still in its earliest stage (pre-docketing); the company had not yet publicly confirmed docket assignment or filed the detailed license application with safety and environmental analyses.

## Claim Verification

### Claim: $900M DOE contract will "supply a significant portion of the nation's advanced nuclear fuel needs"
**Status:** Unverified as delivered supply; contract is a funding vehicle, not proof of production capability

**Supporting:** The contract is real and confirmed via DOE and PR Newswire; it is milestone-based, meaning payment is tied to demonstrated progress rather than paid upfront in full

**Refuting / questioning:** An IDIQ contract sets a ceiling and funding mechanism, not a guarantee of technical success; General Matter has not disclosed its enrichment technology, has no operating enrichment facility, and has not received an NRC license as of last review — the entire $900M program depends on technology and licensing risks that are unproven in public sources

**Summary:** The contract and site lease are real, verifiable commitments of federal funding and DOE facility access. Whether General Matter can actually build and license a working enrichment cascade — the hardest part of the enrichment business historically — is entirely unverified as of this review.

### Claim: Enrichment operations targeted for "late 2020s" with full capacity by 2034
**Status:** Unverified design-stage timeline

**Supporting:** Timeline attributed to General Matter/DOE announcement materials via Wikipedia's sourced summary

**Refuting / questioning:** No NRC license has been issued; historical US enrichment facility licensing and construction (e.g., the never-completed American Centrifuge Plant expansions, Urenco USA's decade-plus buildout) has consistently taken longer than initial industry announcements suggested; General Matter's own regulatory engagement was still at the Letter of Intent stage as recently as December 2024

**Summary:** Consistent with this section's general skepticism toward nuclear-adjacent timeline claims (see Nuclear section overview), the late-2020s-to-2034 timeline should be treated as an aspirational target pending NRC license issuance and confirmed construction start at Paducah.
