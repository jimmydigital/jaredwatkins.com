## Summary

Aalo Atomics is an Austin, Texas microreactor developer founded in 2023 by Matt Loszak (CEO) and Yasir Arafat (President & CTO), the latter the former lead of Idaho National Laboratory's MARVEL microreactor project and a founder of Westinghouse's eVinci programme. Its reactor is a pool-type **sodium-cooled, graphite-moderated** design running on **standard-assay LEU oxide fuel** — deliberately avoiding both HALEU and TRISO, the two fuel forms that constrain most of this section's other advanced reactor developers. The commercial product is the **Aalo Pod**: five 10 MWe Aalo-1 reactors sharing turbines for 50 MWe, marketed specifically at AI datacenter load and requiring no external water source.

On **July 4, 2026** Aalo's **Critical Test Reactor** achieved first criticality at INL — the fourth reactor to go critical under DOE's Reactor Pilot Program, and the one that hit the Executive Order's headline date exactly. The CTR is a **full-scale zero-power critical assembly**, not a power reactor; Aalo's own press materials blur this, and much downstream coverage reported it as a 10 MWe reactor going critical, which it was not. See Claim Verification.

Aalo is distinguished from its criticality-cohort peers by an unusually credentialed team (INL, Westinghouse, TerraPower, SpaceX, Texas PUC alumni), a conventional and comparatively de-risked fuel supply chain (Urenco USA enrichment, Global Nuclear Fuel fabrication, Baker Hughes turbine), and — notably — an almost complete absence of the critical coverage directed at [Valar Atomics]({{< relref "valar-atomics.md" >}}). It is also the company with the largest gap between announced funding ($136M) and claimed funding ($300M+), and has no executed PPA despite three announced customer relationships.

## What It Is / How It Works

**A deliberately boring fuel cycle.** The most consequential design decision Aalo made is not the sodium coolant but the fuel: conventional UO₂ ceramic pellets at standard-to-slightly-elevated LEU enrichment, fabricated by **Global Nuclear Fuel** — GE Vernova's existing commercial fuel alliance — from **Urenco USA** feedstock. This sidesteps both chokepoints this section tracks: the HALEU queue that constrains [Oklo]({{< relref "oklo.md" >}}) and [X-energy]({{< relref "x-energy.md" >}}), and the concentration of US TRISO fabrication on BWXT that [Kairos Power]({{< relref "kairos-power.md" >}}), X-energy, [Radiant Industries]({{< relref "radiant-industries.md" >}}), [NuCube Energy]({{< relref "nucube-energy.md" >}}) and [Antares Nuclear]({{< relref "antares-nuclear.md" >}}) all depend on. Aalo's fuel went from contract signature (March 2026) to delivery (early April 2026) in weeks — a schedule impossible on either constrained pathway. Note that the earlier published Aalo-1 concept used uranium zirconium hydride fuel; the UO₂ design supersedes it.

**Sodium at low temperature, graphite for moderation.** Unlike the sodium **fast** reactors at [TerraPower]({{< relref "terrapower.md" >}}) (Natrium) and Oklo (Aurora), Aalo's design is a **thermal**-spectrum reactor: graphite moderates neutrons while liquid sodium at near-atmospheric pressure carries heat. Sodium's high boiling point removes the need for a pressurized primary system, and the pool configuration provides substantial passive heat capacity. The stated 160–425 °C operating band is low for a sodium system, which reduces materials and chemistry difficulty relative to a fast reactor but caps thermal efficiency and rules out high-temperature process heat. The air-cooled balance of plant — no cooling water — is a specific and increasingly relevant answer to datacenter siting constraints in water-stressed regions.

**The "XMR" packaging argument.** Aalo's commercial unit is not a reactor but a plant: five 10 MWe reactors feeding shared turbines to make 50 MWe. The argument is that microreactor economics fail on per-unit fixed costs — licensing, security, staffing, grid interconnection — and that amortizing those across five cores inside one boundary is what makes small reactors pencil. This is structurally similar in intent to Valar Atomics' "gigasite" thesis, arrived at from the opposite direction (Valar aggregates hundreds of units and sells fuel, Aalo aggregates five and sells electricity).

**What the CTR actually demonstrated.** ANS states it precisely: a "full-scale zero-power version" of the planned 10 MWe Aalo-X. The core geometry, fuel loading, control systems and fueling procedures were validated at commercial **scale**; no power was produced and no electricity generated. Aalo's own claim that criticality "validated our supply chain, reactor physics, control systems, and fueling procedures at commercial scale" is defensible on geometry and procedures, and says nothing about thermal-hydraulic performance, materials behaviour under sustained flux, or power operation — none of which a zero-power assembly can test.

## Claim Verification

### Claim: "Aalo-X Critical Test Reactor (10 MWe)" went critical
**Status:** Misleading as widely reported — the reactor that went critical is a zero-power assembly

**Supporting:** Aalo's own BusinessWire headline reads "Aalo-X Critical Test Reactor" and juxtaposes the 10 MWe figure; Data Center Dynamics reproduced this as "the Aalo-X Critical Test Reactor (10MWe capacity)."

**Refuting / questioning:** ANS states the milestone precisely as Aalo's "critical test reactor — a **full-scale zero-power version** of its planned 10-MWe Aalo-X." POWER is equally explicit: "CTR is zero-power physics reactor… experimental, non-power-producing." No electricity was generated. Aalo-X, the actual 10 MWe power plant, is not yet built.

**Summary:** Full-scale in **geometry**, zero in **power**. This is the single most important distinction in the entry and the one most often lost in secondary coverage. The same caveat applies across the entire 2026 criticality cohort.

### Claim: "$300M+ in venture capital raised"
**Status:** Company-sourced only; unsupported by any announcement

**Supporting:** aalo.com/company currently states "$300M+ in venture capital."

**Refuting / questioning:** The last announced round is the $100M Series B of August 2025, after which the company itself stated "over $136 million" total (BusinessWire) and Sacra reported $133M. No press release or trade-press report of any round between August 2025 and August 2026 was found. A third-party marks platform lists a $450M valuation, also unverified.

**Summary:** Likely an unannounced Series C, but this cannot be confirmed. Cite $136M as the last confirmed total and note the $300M+ claim as company-sourced.

### Claim: "Microsoft-backed"
**Status:** Unsubstantiated characterization

**Supporting:** Data Center Dynamics labels Aalo "Microsoft-backed," and Microsoft does appear in Aalo materials.

**Refuting / questioning:** DCD's own text concedes "specific investment details aren't disclosed." Microsoft is not in any disclosed cap table. The only confirmed Microsoft relationship is a **technical collaboration** with Microsoft and Nvidia on an automated co-piloting system for reactor safety — not an investment and not an offtake.

**Summary:** Do not describe Microsoft as an Aalo investor.

### Claim: Construction start date and "under eight months" build
**Status:** Unresolved — three incompatible dates

**Supporting:** WNN reports construction began August 2025 (groundbreaking to criticality under eight months); NEI reports September 2025 with a 36-day building and 40-day reactor assembly; POWER reports January 2026 with 36-day building, 28-day Austin fabrication, and 540 fuel rods assembled in 2.5 days.

**Refuting / questioning:** The company's "less than eight months" claim is consistent with an Aug–Nov 2025 start and inconsistent with POWER's January 2026 date — yet POWER's account is the most granularly sourced.

**Summary:** Unresolved. The build was fast by any of these accounts; the specific figure should not be cited without qualification.

### Claim: Operating temperature
**Status:** Sources disagree

**Supporting:** aalo.com states 160–425 °C at near-atmospheric pressure; NucNet's SMR database lists a 550 °C outlet.

**Summary:** Unresolved. The distinction matters for whether Aalo can serve industrial process heat or is electricity-only.

## Risk & Skepticism Notes

- **Every customer relationship is non-binding.** Crusoe is a "strategic partnership" with intent language and no disclosed PPA; Texas A&M RELLIS is a selection to explore "up to 1 GW"; NRG Energy is a Series B investor, not an offtaker. Aalo's stated business model is selling electricity under long-term PPAs, and **not one has been executed**.
- **No NRC application confirmed.** A COLA was targeted for 2026 and leadership said in March 2026 they planned to "submit our first commercial license this year," but no submission could be confirmed. As ANS puts it for the whole programme: "without NRC approval, none of the companies involved will be able to deploy their designs commercially."
- **Timeline internally inconsistent.** ANS reports Aalo-X facility completion end-2026 while aalo.com says Q1 2027; the Crusoe release targets Aalo-X **powering** a datacenter in 2027 while POWER frames Aalo-X as "construction/licensing 2027, operations 2028."
- **Unusually little critical coverage.** ANS's own assessment of its criticality reporting: "None detected. The article presents Aalo's achievement without critical analysis." Aalo has attracted none of the founder-credential, radiation-claim or investor-tie scrutiny directed at Valar Atomics. That absence is a gap in the record, not evidence of soundness — and Katy Huff's critique of zero-power criticality ("simply checks if you can multiply neutrons") applies to Aalo exactly as it does to Valar.
- **A sodium thermal reactor is still first-of-a-kind.** Conventional fuel does not make the reactor conventional: graphite-moderated pool-type sodium systems have no operating commercial precedent, and graphite behaviour under long-term flux in a sodium environment is the kind of materials question a zero-power assembly cannot answer.
