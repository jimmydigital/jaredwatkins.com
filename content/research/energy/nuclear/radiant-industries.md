## Summary

Radiant Industries (operating as Radiant Nuclear) is a Los Angeles microreactor developer founded in 2020 by CEO Doug Bernauer, a 12-year SpaceX veteran who worked on Grasshopper avionics and Mars power concepts, and CTO/co-founder Bob Urberger. The company's Kaleidos is a portable, assembly-line-manufactured microreactor targeting 1+ MWe output using TRISO fuel and helium cooling, designed to operate for years without refueling and to be deployed wherever grid power is impractical — remote sites, disaster response, military installations, and data centers. Radiant has raised over $525 million cumulatively across four rounds (Series A through D, the latest a $300M+ Series D in December 2025), and is, by funding and regulatory progress, the most advanced company in DOE's Nuclear Energy Launch Pad cohort: it was the first new US reactor design selected for testing in DOE's DOME test bed at Idaho National Laboratory, received DOE's first-ever full-power microreactor test authorization in February 2026, and became the first microreactor company to advance to NRC Part 70 license review (accepted May 2026) for its planned production facility in Oak Ridge, Tennessee. Commercial commitments include a 20-unit preorder from Equinix and a US Air Force contract to deliver Kaleidos units to Buckley Space Force Base, Colorado, by 2028.

## What It Is / How It Works

**Portable, factory-built, TRISO-fueled:** Kaleidos is designed from the outset as a manufactured product rather than a site-built plant — the explicit goal is an assembly-line process that can turn out up to 50 units per year once the Oak Ridge R-50 facility is operating at scale. TRISO (tri-structural isotropic) particle fuel is the same fuel form used by Kairos Power and X-energy elsewhere in this research section, chosen across the advanced-reactor industry for its inherent containment of fission products within each fuel particle's ceramic coating layers, which supports passive safety claims without relying on active cooling systems. Helium cooling avoids water-reactivity and pressurization concerns associated with water-cooled designs.

**DOME as shared federal test infrastructure:** DOME (Demonstration of Microreactor Experiments) is a DOE/NRIC test bed at Idaho National Laboratory built specifically to let private microreactor developers test full designs without each company needing to build independent federal test infrastructure. Radiant's selection as the first new US reactor design tested there (the facility's first use in nearly 50 years for a genuinely new domestic reactor design, per company materials) is a significant sequencing advantage — the DARK safety authorization in February 2026 was the first full-power microreactor test authorization DOE has ever issued, ahead of the other Launch Pad and Reactor Pilot Program companies.

**Regulatory lead over peers:** Radiant's R-50 Part 70 license application, accepted by the NRC in May 2026, makes it the first microreactor company in the Launch Pad cohort — and among all companies tracked in this research section — to reach formal NRC license review for a production facility, as distinct from a one-off DOE-authorized demonstration unit. The application specifically covers the fueling building at Oak Ridge, a necessary step because Radiant's business model depends on fueling reactors at the factory before shipment, unlike site-fueled designs.

**Defense and hyperscaler dual-track commercialization:** Radiant is pursuing defense installations (Buckley Space Force Base, under the Air Force's ANPI program) and data center operators (Equinix's 20-unit preorder) as parallel initial markets — both value grid-independent, rapidly deployable power, and both can absorb higher per-unit costs during early production than commodity grid-scale power would allow.

## Claim Verification

### Claim: Kaleidos will deliver "more than 1 MW" for "years without refueling," fully portable and grid-independent
**Status:** Partially verified — the design specifications are consistently reported, but no Kaleidos unit has yet operated commercially or completed its first full-power test as of last review

**Supporting:** DOE's February 2026 DARK approval is real, independently confirmed DOE authorization for full-power testing — a genuine, verifiable regulatory milestone distinguishing Radiant from companies still awaiting DOE test authorization; the design specifications (>1 MWe, TRISO fuel, helium cooling, multi-year refueling) are consistent across company materials, DOE announcements, and independent press coverage

**Refuting / questioning:** As of this review, Kaleidos has not completed a full-power operational test — the DOME startup was targeted for summer 2026, and no independent post-test performance data was available in sources reviewed; "years without refueling" and portability claims remain design targets pending demonstrated field operation

**Summary:** Radiant's regulatory and funding progress is unusually well-documented and verifiable relative to peers in this section, but the core performance claims (output, refueling interval, portability under real operating conditions) remain unverified pending the DOME full-power test results.

### Claim: 50 reactors/year manufacturing capacity by 2028
**Status:** Unverified design-stage target

**Supporting:** The R-50 facility is a real, funded (Series D-backed) production facility under NRC Part 70 review for its fueling building — a concrete regulatory and capital commitment beyond a bare marketing claim

**Refuting / questioning:** No nuclear reactor manufacturer — of any design type, at any company — has yet demonstrated true assembly-line production of multiple reactors per year; the closest historical analogues (naval reactor programs) operated at far lower cadence under different regulatory and cost structures; Radiant's own commercial deployment target of "2028" for first customer deliveries implies the 50-unit/year target is a scale-up milestone beyond that, with limited public detail on the ramp timeline

**Summary:** The manufacturing ambition is credible in terms of funding and site development but remains an unproven claim consistent with this section's general skepticism toward nuclear construction/manufacturing timeline claims — track first-unit delivery in 2028 as the leading indicator before assessing scale-up credibility.
