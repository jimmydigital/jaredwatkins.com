## Summary

Antares Nuclear is a Torrance, California microreactor developer founded in 2023 by Jordan Bramble. On **June 4, 2026** its Mark-0 demonstration reactor achieved initial criticality at Idaho National Laboratory's Materials and Fuels Complex — the **first** reactor to go critical under DOE's Reactor Pilot Program, ahead of Valar Atomics, Deployable Energy, Aalo Atomics and Oklo. The reactor is a **sodium heat-pipe-cooled, graphite-moderated** design running TRISO fuel at 19.75% HALEU enrichment, with no pumps in the primary heat path.

Antares is the most defence-oriented company in this section. Its customer set is entirely governmental: the Air Force ANPI initiative (Joint Base San Antonio, April 2026), the Army's **Janus program** (Fort Bragg, August 2026), and a stated NASA lunar fission surface power ambition. It has raised **>$600M across four rounds**, most recently a **$470M Series C** in July 2026 co-led by Paradigm and Caffeinated Capital, and in May 2026 signed what is billed as the world's first multi-year **commercial HALEU supply agreement**, with Urenco.

Three things distinguish it from its criticality-cohort peers: it went first; its regulatory position is arguably the thinnest of the group (an NRC pre-application docket with a single "no review requested" engagement plan); and its Army work will be regulated by the **Army**, not the NRC or DOE — a third regime that sits outside civilian nuclear oversight entirely.

## What It Is / How It Works

**Heat pipes instead of pumps.** Antares' core engineering claim is a primary heat-transport path with no moving parts. Sealed steel tubes containing liquid sodium run from the core outward; reactor heat vaporizes sodium at the hot end, the vapor travels to a heat exchanger, condenses, and returns to the core by **capillary action through an internal wick**. There is no pump, no valve, and no external power requirement in that loop, so the company argues cooling continues through a total station blackout. Combined with TRISO fuel — where each fuel kernel carries its own ceramic containment — the pitch is a reactor that is difficult to melt down and can run autonomously for years without refueling, in a package small enough to truck or airlift.

This is the same passive-heat-pipe concept pursued by [NuCube Energy]({{< relref "nucube-energy.md" >}}) in this section and by Westinghouse's eVinci, though implementations differ. The recurring technical objections are also common to the class: graphite moderators crack under sustained neutron flux, and sodium heat pipes scale poorly to higher power — which is why heat-pipe designs cluster at the sub-megawatt end and why the economics at that scale remain unproven.

**The defence market is the whole thesis.** Where [Oklo]({{< relref "oklo.md" >}}), [Aalo Atomics]({{< relref "aalo-atomics.md" >}}) and [X-energy]({{< relref "x-energy.md" >}}) are chasing hyperscaler datacenter load, Antares is building for forward operating bases, installation resilience and — eventually — lunar surface power. The customer has different requirements: transportability, autonomous operation with minimal staffing, tolerance for higher cost per kilowatt-hour, and indifference to grid interconnection. It also has a different regulatory posture, which is the most consequential structural fact about this company.

**Three separate regulatory regimes, none of them commercial NRC licensing.** Mark-0 ran under **DOE** authorization (PDSA → DSA → readiness review → startup approval under DOE-STD-1271). The Army's Janus reactors will be **Army-regulated**, contractor-owned and contractor-operated. And **NRC** — the independent civilian safety regulator — has from Antares only a pre-application engagement plan submitted with "no review requested." Executive Order 14299 mandates a reactor operating at a domestic military installation by September 30, 2028, and the Janus OTA structure is designed to reach that date. Whatever one concludes about the merits, none of these pathways produces an NRC licence, and Antares would need one to sell power to a civilian customer.

**What criticality demonstrated.** Very little, by the company's own reactor's construction. Mark-0 has no power conversion and no heat removal system; INL's director confirmed there was "essentially no measurable energy output" and that "this is not electricity generation." It validated core physics, neutronics and instrumentation. Edwin Lyman of the Union of Concerned Scientists was blunter: "This stunt is a rudimentary first step that has absolutely no bearing on whether the Antares reactor will be safe or commercially viable," and specifically disputed DOE's characterization that the test "confirms the reactor can operate safely."

**⚑ A three-year fuel gap.** Antares targets first production deployments in **2028**, but its commercial HALEU contract draws on a Urenco UK facility that does not open until **2031**. Bramble's own framing acknowledges the bridge — the Urenco deal covers "when we scale beyond material allocated by the federal government." Until then Antares depends on DOE-allocated HALEU downblended from NNSA scrap, a finite and politically allocated resource that [Oklo]({{< relref "oklo.md" >}}), [X-energy]({{< relref "x-energy.md" >}}) and [Kairos Power]({{< relref "kairos-power.md" >}}) are also drawing against. **⚑ BWXT concentration deepens.** Antares joins Kairos, X-energy, [Radiant Industries]({{< relref "radiant-industries.md" >}}) and NuCube in depending on BWXT's Lynchburg TRISO line — and separately competes with **BWXT Advanced Technologies** for Army Janus work (BWXT won Fort Campbell). BWXT is simultaneously Antares' sole fuel fabricator and a direct competitor for its primary customer.

**⚑ Two different Urenco entities in this section.** Antares contracts for **HALEU from Urenco's UK plant**; [Deep Fission]({{< relref "deep-fission.md" >}}) contracts for **LEU from Urenco USA** (Eunice, NM); [Aalo Atomics]({{< relref "aalo-atomics.md" >}}) also uses Urenco USA. ## Claim Verification

### Claim: Mark-0's criticality demonstrated the reactor "can operate safely"
**Status:** Disputed — DOE's characterization was challenged directly

**Supporting:** DOE announced the milestone as the first advanced reactor criticality under the Reactor Pilot Program and framed it as confirming safe operation.

**Refuting / questioning:** INL Director John Wagner stated the reaction "was sustained at essentially no measurable energy output. **This is not electricity generation.**" Mark-0 has no power conversion system and no heat removal system at all. Edwin Lyman (Union of Concerned Scientists) called the test "a rudimentary first step that has absolutely no bearing on whether the Antares reactor will be safe or commercially viable," and specifically disputed DOE's safety characterization as false. DOE Assistant Secretary Ted Garrish publicly acknowledged programme skeptics at the announcement.

**Summary:** Criticality validated core physics and instrumentation. It says nothing about thermal performance, materials behaviour, or safety under power — and the DOE framing overstates it.

### Claim: R1 power rating, deployment dates, and total funding
**Status:** Company-sourced only

**Supporting:** All figures — 100 kW–1 MWe for R1, "years without refueling," ~10 units/yr Torrance capacity, ">$600M total raised," the 2027 Mark-1 and 2028 deployment dates — come from Antares press releases.

**Refuting / questioning:** No independent verification exists for any of them. The **thermal rating (MWt) of every Antares variant is undisclosed**, as is the valuation of every funding round, the identity of the co-founders, the board, the Urenco contract terms, and the per-company Janus dollar figure.

**Summary:** The funding rounds themselves are well documented by law-firm and newswire records; the technical and schedule claims are not independently verified.

### Claim: Janus and ANPI are contracts
**Status:** Both are pre-contractual instruments

**Supporting:** Both are real, competitive government selections with named installations.

**Refuting / questioning:** The Air Force ANPI selection is explicitly for **proposed** deployment, not a finalized contract. The Army Janus awards are **Other Transaction Authority agreements** with **milestone-gated** funding that the Army may reallocate between vendors — not firm production contracts. The $2.2B figure is the combined five-vendor ceiling over FY2027–2031, not an Antares award.

**Summary:** Real government traction, but do not cite a dollar figure for Antares or describe either as a production contract.

## Risk & Skepticism Notes

- **The thinnest NRC position in the cohort.** Antares' entire NRC file is a pre-application docket opened May 2025 and one Regulatory Engagement Plan submitted with "no review requested." Nothing has been applied for. Its three operating pathways — DOE authorization, Army regulation, and eventual NRC licensing — currently include exactly zero NRC instruments.
- **Army self-regulation is a genuinely novel arrangement.** Janus reactors will be contractor-owned, contractor-operated and **Army-regulated**, outside both NRC civilian licensing and the DOE authorization framework. There is no established precedent for commercial microreactors operating on this basis, and it is the mechanism by which the September 30, 2028 EO 14299 deadline becomes reachable.
- **No commercial customer.** Every Antares relationship is with the federal government. There is no PPA, no datacenter agreement, and no civilian offtake — which means the company's revenue path runs entirely through programmes whose funding is milestone-gated and politically contingent.
- **The 2031 fuel gap** (above) is the concrete near-term execution risk.
- **Undisclosed basics.** No valuation, no thermal rating, no board, no named co-founders. For a company that has raised over $600M, this is an unusual amount of opacity.
