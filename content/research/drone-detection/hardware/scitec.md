## Summary

SciTec, Inc. is a Princeton, New Jersey–based national-security technology company, founded 1979 (45+ years of operation as of 2025), specializing in remote sensing software, missile warning/tracking, intelligence-surveillance-reconnaissance (ISR) data fusion, space domain awareness, and command-and-control (C2) systems for the US Department of Defense, Space Force, and intelligence community. On October 5, 2025, Firefly Aerospace (Nasdaq: FLY) announced a definitive agreement to acquire SciTec for approximately $855 million ($300M cash plus $555M in Firefly stock); the deal closed November 5, 2025, and SciTec now operates as a Firefly subsidiary. **Editorial note on placement:** SciTec's core business is missile warning, ISR, space domain awareness, and C2 — not primarily drones. It is filed in this section because its public materials cite "machine learning for counter-UAS technology" as one of its national-security applications; readers should treat SciTec as a defense sensing/software company with a peripheral counter-UAS angle, not a dedicated C-UAS vendor. Do not confuse this company with the similarly named [SCI Technology, Inc.]({{< relref "sci-technology-aeroguard.md" >}}) (Sanmina subsidiary), maker of the AeroGuard drone-interdiction system.

## What It Is / How It Works

SciTec's business is organized around what it calls the "SciTec Triad": system modeling (predicting and simulating threats before they occur), rapid prototyping (fast design-build-test cycles), and operational evolution (iterating fielded systems against emerging threats). Its five named capability areas are: (1) missile warning, tracking, and defense — including ground- and space-based sensor data exploitation; (2) intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) data fusion; (3) command and control (C2), including automated multi-sensor/multi-domain data correlation for Joint All-Domain Command and Control (JADC2); (4) space domain awareness; and (5) aircraft survivability / countermeasure test equipment for rotary- and fixed-wing aircraft. The company's site names "machine learning for counter-UAS technology" as an application of its broader sensor-fusion and target-detection software stack, but does not describe a discrete counter-UAS hardware product — this differs structurally from hardware-first C-UAS vendors in this section (DroneShield, Dedrone, Fortem) that sell dedicated detection/interdiction systems.

A major named program is SciTec's role in the Future Operational Resilient Ground Evolution (FORGE) framework, where the company was awarded a $259 million US Space Force contract (announced 2025) to deliver scalable, cyber-secure ground processing for the Space Force's missile warning and tracking mission. SciTec's software is also positioned as a contributor to the Trump administration's proposed "Golden Dome" missile defense architecture, per statements from both SciTec and Firefly Aerospace leadership.
