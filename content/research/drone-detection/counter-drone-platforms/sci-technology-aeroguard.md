## Summary

SCI Technology, Inc. is a Huntsville, Alabama–based defense and aerospace electronics manufacturer, operating since 1961 (originally founded as Space Craft, Incorporated) and a wholly owned subsidiary of Sanmina Corporation (Nasdaq: SANM) since Sanmina's 2001 acquisition of SCI Systems. Among its counter-UAS product line, **AeroGuard** is a fully autonomous, rapid-response drone interdiction system that physically nets and tows away intruding unmanned aircraft under FAA Part 107 thresholds, without RF jamming. **Editorial note on naming confusion:** SCI Technology (sci.com) is a distinct company from [SciTec, Inc.]({{< relref "../hardware/scitec.md" >}}) (scitec.com, a Princeton, NJ missile-warning/ISR contractor recently acquired by Firefly Aerospace) — the similar names are a frequent source of confusion in defense-industry search results and this entry should not be merged with that one.

## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 1961 (as Space Craft, Incorporated, by Olin King); rebranded SCI Technology in 2012
- **HQ:** Huntsville, Alabama (685,000 sq. ft. facility on a 100-acre campus)
- **Type:** Private; wholly owned subsidiary of Sanmina Corporation (Nasdaq: SANM) since 2001 acquisition (Sanmina acquired SCI Systems for ~$6B in cash, stock, and debt)
- **Business model:** Platform OEM (AeroGuard system) and defense/aerospace electronics manufacturer (broader SCI Technology business: aircraft intercoms, data concentrators, fiber optics, integrated manufacturing services)
- **AeroGuard product status:** Named 2018 Drone Security New Product of the Year by Security Today magazine; US and foreign patents pending as of company materials; ITAR-free, ECCN 9A991.b classification
- **Primary market for AeroGuard:** Large outdoor gatherings (sporting events, concerts), critical infrastructure, contraband/IP-piracy prevention — explicitly positioned as a **civilian-legal** alternative to RF jamming

## What It Is / How It Works

AeroGuard is a net-capture drone interdiction system built around a Command & Control (C2) hub and a fleet of autonomous, dock-based Interceptor drones. The described operational sequence: a third-party drone detection system (radar, EO/IR, acoustic, or other sensor) identifies an intruding UAV and alerts AeroGuard's C2 system; C2 selects and launches available Interceptors within seconds; each Interceptor computes an intercept path from real-time C2 updates; an onboard terminal guidance system calculates a firing solution and net-captures the target; the captured drone is towed to a designated disposal zone while the Interceptor returns to its dock to recharge. SCI Technology markets AeroGuard's central differentiator as **no RF jamming** — the system does not interfere with cell phones, WiFi, or other legal communications, which the company positions as making it deployable by civilian security operators (not just federal agencies subject to jamming-authorization restrictions under the FAA Reauthorization Act; see this section's [Regulatory Framework]({{< relref "../regulatory-framework.md" >}}) entry). AeroGuard integrates with existing CCTV and third-party detection sensors (radar, EO/IR, acoustic) rather than shipping its own primary sensor suite, positioning it as an interdiction layer that sits atop a customer's existing detection stack.

SCI Technology's broader business (aircraft intercoms/FireComm, data concentrators, fiber optics, IWICS/ISWICS wireless intercommunication, integrated manufacturing services) is a legacy Sanmina defense-electronics contract manufacturing operation with roots in the Apollo program and Voyager 2 — AeroGuard is one specific counter-UAS product line within a much larger, diversified defense-manufacturing business, not the company's primary revenue driver.

## Claim Verification

### Claim: AeroGuard operates without RF jamming and is "civilian-friendly"/legally deployable outside federal authorization

**Status:** Partially verified (company-stated design characteristic; legal deployability depends on jurisdiction-specific rules for physical drone capture, not just the jamming question)

**Supporting sources:**
- [AeroGuard — SCI Technology product page](https://www.sci.com/aeroguard/) — company states "No illegal RF jamming to interfere with cell phones, WiFi or any other legal communications systems"

**Refuting / questioning sources:**
- None found directly addressing AeroGuard; however, this section's own [Regulatory Framework]({{< relref "../regulatory-framework.md" >}}) and [Property Owner Rights vs. Drone Operator Rules]({{< relref "../property-owner-rights-and-operator-rules.md" >}}) entries note that physical interdiction/capture of another party's aircraft — even without RF jamming — can separately implicate FAA airspace authority, state/local self-help laws, and liability for damage to the captured drone, regardless of whether jamming is involved. The absence of RF jamming addresses one legal constraint but does not by itself establish that net-capture is unrestricted for non-federal operators in all jurisdictions.

**Summary:** AeroGuard's no-jamming design is confirmed by the company's own materials and is a real technical distinction from RF-based mitigation systems. Whether that alone makes physical interception broadly civilian-legal (as opposed to merely avoiding the specific jamming-authorization statute) was not independently verified against current FAA/state law in this review; treat SCI Technology's "civilian-friendly" framing as a marketing characterization pending independent legal confirmation.
