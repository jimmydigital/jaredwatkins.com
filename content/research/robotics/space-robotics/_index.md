## Overview

Space robotics tracks two structurally distinct company types that both build robots "in space" but operate in very different markets. The first is the mechanisms/tools layer — companies building robotic arms, drills, and sample-handling hardware for planetary exploration and in-space servicing, where platforms operate in vacuum, extreme temperature swings, radiation, and regolith/dust with no possibility of field repair, and typically fly only once. Here the competitive moat is decades of flight heritage and qualification, not manufacturing scale — a handful of companies with 1980s-vintage roots (Honeybee Robotics) still dominate specific mechanism categories supplied to NASA and, increasingly, commercial lunar missions. The second is autonomous orbital defense spacecraft — companies (True Anomaly) building complete maneuverable vehicles and mission-autonomy software for rendezvous-and-proximity operations, space-domain awareness, and orbital "space superiority" missions, funded by and sold almost exclusively to the US Space Force and allied defense customers. These two segments share almost no supply chain or customer overlap beyond both operating beyond Earth's atmosphere; they are grouped here only because no other subsection fits either, not because they compete or partner.

## Key Themes

- Flight heritage is the primary barrier to entry in the mechanisms/tools segment — few new entrants can credibly compete against decades of qualified hardware history on Mars/Moon missions
- Consolidation into larger space primes: legacy independent space-robotics specialists are increasingly acquired by launch/lander companies (Blue Origin, and Firefly-adjacent primes) seeking vertical integration of mechanisms and tools
- NASA CLPS (Commercial Lunar Payload Services) program is driving a new wave of lunar rover and instrument-deployment contracts for space-robotics suppliers
- Orbital defense spacecraft is an emerging, fast-moving, defense-only category distinct from the mechanisms/tools segment — driven almost entirely by US Space Force demand signal (Andromeda, Space-Based Interceptor, VICTUS-series responsive-space programs) rather than commercial/NASA science missions
- Dual-use overlap with defense robotics and medical device mechanisms — several space-robotics mechanisms firms cross-apply drill/actuator IP to non-space markets

## Supply Chain

Space-robotics mechanisms depend on the same precision-actuator and motor supply chain documented in [Actuators]({{< relref "../actuators/_index.md" >}}) (harmonic drives, brushless motors) but with an additional layer of radiation-hardened electronics and space-qualified materials (non-outgassing lubricants, thermal-cycling-rated fasteners) not required for terrestrial robotics.
