## Summary

Honeybee Robotics is a space and extreme-environment robotics engineering company founded in 1983 by Stephen Gorevan and Chris Chapman, originally as a New York City–based systems integrator using off-the-shelf robots. Since 1983 it has completed more than 1,000 projects for NASA, the US Department of Defense, academia, and industry, and its mechanisms — most notably the Rock Abrasion Tools on the Mars Exploration Rovers (Spirit and Opportunity) and the sample-acquisition drills on Curiosity and Perseverance — have flown on every NASA Mars surface mission of the past two decades. The company was acquired by Blue Origin in a deal announced January 24, 2022 and closed in mid-February 2022, becoming a wholly owned Blue Origin subsidiary while retaining its own brand, leadership, and operations "as usual." Honeybee is now supplying the lunar rover for Firefly Aerospace's NASA-awarded mission to the Gruithuisen Domes, targeted to launch aboard a Firefly Blue Ghost lander.

- **Track record:** 1,000+ completed projects since 1983 across spacecraft, planetary exploration, defense robotics, medical devices, mining, oil & gas, and utility infrastructure
- **Value Chain Layer:** Component/subsystem supplier (historically), expanding into Platform OEM for specific missions (lunar rover)

## What It Is / How It Works

Honeybee's core competency is engineering mechanisms that must operate reliably, unrepaired, in vacuum, extreme thermal cycling, radiation, and abrasive dust — conditions that make conventional terrestrial robotics components (lubricants, bearings, connectors) unsuitable without extensive redesign and qualification. Its flight heritage includes the Rock Abrasion Tool (RAT) on the Mars Exploration Rovers Spirit and Opportunity (2004), the Icy Sample Acquisition Device on the Phoenix Mars Lander, and the sample-acquisition drill systems on Curiosity (Mars Science Laboratory) and Perseverance. Beyond drills and sampling tools, the company's stated capability set spans robotic arms, manipulators, deployment booms, motors, actuators, drive electronics, and other specialized space mechanisms — component-level hardware historically sold or contracted to prime contractors and NASA centers rather than sold as complete end-user platforms.

Since joining Blue Origin, Honeybee has been positioned within Blue Origin's Exploration Systems business, supporting the company's stated goal of "making space flight routine" and enabling Blue Moon lunar lander and related systems. A notable post-acquisition expansion beyond its historical component-supplier role: Firefly Aerospace selected Honeybee to build the complete lunar rover for a NASA CLPS task order to explore the Gruithuisen Domes on the Moon's near side, targeted for a 2028 mission. The rover will be deployed by Firefly's Blue Ghost lander and will carry NASA science instruments to investigate the unusual, silica-rich composition of the domes — a geologically distinct feature thought to require more evolved (non-basaltic) lunar volcanism to form.
