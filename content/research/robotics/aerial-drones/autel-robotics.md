## Summary

Autel Robotics is a Shenzhen-based drone maker that competed with DJI in the prosumer and enterprise segments and operates a US subsidiary in Bothell, Washington. It accumulated the same US designations as DJI: the Commerce Entity List (reported mid-June 2024), the DoD Section 1260H list (Federal Register, 2025-01-07) and, on 2025-12-22, the FCC Covered List, when the FCC added all foreign-made drones and components. That action blocks new models; previously authorized models can still be imported, sold and flown. In July 2025 Autel announced it was retiring its Evo Nano and Evo Lite consumer lines (support ends 2030-07-18) to focus on professional and enterprise drones. On 2026-01-21 it filed an Application for Review with the FCC.

## What It Is / How It Works

Autel built folding camera drones for cinematographers, public safety and commercial operators. After the consumer exit, TechRadar describes the focus as professional and enterprise drones without detailing that line.

**Regulatory position.** The FCC's December 2025 action covers new equipment authorizations; as of July 2026 previously authorized DJI and Autel models were still legal to buy and fly. DroneXL notes the FCC could extend its import-ban mechanism to the December 2025 additions through a public notice, which had not happened as of 2026-07-05. Conditional approvals for eleven non-Chinese manufacturers run to 2026-12-31. DoD opposed removing DJI from the list; the sources reviewed do not report the FCC's decision on Autel's petition.
