## Summary

Joby Aviation (NYSE: JOBY) develops the S4, a piloted electric tilt-rotor air taxi. Its Q2 2026 report (2026-08-05) cited its strongest quarterly progress yet in the fifth and final stage of FAA type certification, five aircraft flying with 12 more in production, and $2.3B of cash and short-term investments at 2026-06-30. Blade, which Joby acquired, produced $36.2M of Q2 revenue and 2026 revenue guidance was raised to $115–125M. Joby expects first eIPP flights in Texas in September 2026 and targets first passengers in 2026; it expects second-half 2026 cash use of $385–415M.

## What It Is / How It Works

The S4 takes off vertically on tilting propellers and cruises wing-borne. Joby's FAA path has stages; the August 2025 quarter report (ZAG) described Joby as 70% through Stage Four on its side with the first conforming TIA aircraft in final assembly, and the 2026 report describes progress in the fifth stage. The sources reviewed do not give a type certificate date.

**Commercial path.** Blade's passenger business, bought in 2025 (per the August 2025 report), gives Joby revenue and urban corridors now; the eIPP gives early US flights. ZAG's 2025 summary also described a Dubai flight campaign and a partnership with L3Harris on a turbine-electric defense variant; neither was covered in the 2026 page reviewed, and no Dubai commercial launch date was found.
