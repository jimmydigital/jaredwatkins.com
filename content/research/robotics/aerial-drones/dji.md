## Summary

SZ DJI Technology Co., Ltd. is a Shenzhen-based **Platform OEM** that dominates the commercial and consumer drone market: a 2025 market report put its global commercial share at about 70% ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/04/08/3057358/0/en/Connected-Commercial-Drones-Report-2025-Asia-Pacific-Leads-in-Drone-Adoption-with-DJI-Holding-a-Dominant-70-Global-Market-Share.html)). It was founded in 2006 by Frank Wang and remains private. **Regulatory status is the defining fact for U.S. users:** the FCC added DJI to its Covered List on 2025-12-22, which blocks U.S. equipment authorization for new DJI models; the Pentagon lists DJI as a Chinese military company under Section 1260H, and an August 2026 D.C. Circuit decision sent that designation back to the district court without removing DJI from the list; DJI's Ninth Circuit petition on the FCC action is paused pending an FCC ruling on its reconsideration petition, with DJI's opening brief due 2026-11-02. DJI continues to launch products elsewhere (the Agras T55 and T100 in July 2026; the Osmo Pocket 4P in China, Japan and Europe with no U.S. launch announced).

> **Regulatory flags (steering requirement):** DoD Section 1260H Chinese military company list; FCC Covered List; American Security Drone Act of 2023 (federal procurement and operation); not on the Blue UAS list. Rare-earth (NdFeB) dependency applies to all motors and gimbals.

## What It Is / How It Works

DJI operates as a platform OEM at the top of the robotics value chain, designing, manufacturing, and selling complete, ready-to-use drone systems to consumer and enterprise customers. Unlike component suppliers (motor makers, sensor manufacturers), DJI controls the entire stack: airframe design, flight control firmware, camera systems, gimbal stabilization, image transmission, and software/cloud services.

The company's market dominance rests on three competitive pillars: **vertical integration** (allowing rapid product iteration and cost control), **proprietary transmission technology** (OcuSync/O4+ delivering extended range and low-latency video), and **gimbal/camera innovation** (Hasselblad partnership in Mavic line; internal gimbal R&D since the 2012 Zenmuse Z15 launch).

**Flight control and stabilization:** DJI designs its own flight controller boards and stabilization algorithms. Gimbal systems use brushless motors and gyroscopic feedback to counteract camera movement; DJI's ActiveTrack subject-tracking feature is a manufacturer-described capability.

**Image transmission — OcuSync and O4+:** The proprietary OcuSync system uses custom silicon designed by DJI and operates on 5 GHz bands with H.265 video encoding. DJI claims O4+ range of up to 18.6 miles (30 km) in ideal conditions; ranges differ by regulatory region and are manufacturer claims, not independently measured.

**Vertical integration advantage:** DJI designs much of its flight control, gimbal, camera and transmission stack in-house; DJI-stated self-sufficiency percentages and component-level figures are not independently verified and are omitted here.

**Product positioning:** The Mavic line targets consumer and prosumer videography; Matrice targets professional mapping, inspection, and emergency response; Agras targets precision agriculture. Each line is purpose-built with dedicated sensors, payloads, and software.

## Supply Chain Position

DJI is a **Platform OEM** (Layer 7) with backward integration into components (flight control, gimbals, cameras, image transmission), selling to consumer, enterprise and agricultural customers.

**Rare earth dependency:** DJI's brushless propulsion and gimbal motors are assumed to use NdFeB permanent magnets, as is standard for BLDC motors; DJI does not disclose magnet sourcing. China dominates rare-earth magnet supply, so this is a systemic exposure across the product line.

**Manufacturing concentration:** DJI manufacturing is reported to be in Shenzhen and Dongguan; no overseas assembly sites were identified in sources reviewed. Previous claims about specific suppliers (battery subsidiaries, foundry names) could not be verified and are removed.

**Regulatory effects on the chain:** The Covered List and FCC 26-50 limit new U.S. authorizations for DJI hardware; the 1260H listing and the American Security Drone Act restrict federal customers and contractors. International markets remain open, and DJI is staging product launches outside the U.S.
