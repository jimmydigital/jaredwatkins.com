## Summary

Stratom, Inc. is a privately held, Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB) headquartered in Louisville, Colorado (Boulder metro area), founded in 2001 to develop autonomous ground vehicles and robotic systems for defense and commercial logistics customers. The company builds unmanned cargo-handling and refueling robots — the Autonomous Pallet Loader (APL), eXpeditionary Cargo Loader (XCL), and RAPID autonomous refueling/recharging/liquid-transfer system — alongside the Summit off-road autonomy platform and toolkits (Kobra, ASP, Route Clearance) that add manipulation and payload capability to third-party unmanned ground vehicles. Its customers span the US Army, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Navy, as well as commercial distribution and aviation clients.

## What It Is / How It Works

Stratom's product line centers on removing soldiers and warehouse/ramp workers from repetitive, hazardous material-handling tasks. The **Autonomous Pallet Loader (APL)** and **eXpeditionary Cargo Loader (XCL)** are robotic material-handling systems for military logistics — XCL was demonstrated loading cargo onto a C-130 for Air Force tactical airlift operations in May 2026. **RAPID** (Robotic Autonomous Fueling, Recharging and liquid transfer) automates hazardous liquid transfer — refueling and recharging unmanned systems and vehicles without direct human contact with fuel lines. The **Summit** off-road autonomy platform is a retrofit autonomy kit intended to convert conventional or purpose-built ground vehicles into unmanned platforms for resupply and route-clearance missions. Stratom also sells accessory toolkits — the **Kobra Tool and Payload Kit**, **ASP Toolkit**, and **Route Clearance Toolkit** — that add manipulator arms and mission payloads to existing UGVs such as those historically built by iRobot/Endeavor Robotics and QinetiQ, both listed among its clients.

Stratom's engineering approach for newer defense programs is built around a Modular Open Systems Approach (MOSA) and Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE), intended to keep its robotic platforms upgradeable and interoperable with prime-contractor systems rather than locking customers into a single vendor's proprietary stack.

## Notable Developments

- **2026-04-29:** Awarded US Army contract to develop the Autonomous Decontamination System (ADS) — a robotic platform for remote/semi-autonomous/fully autonomous CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) vehicle washdown, intended to keep soldiers at standoff distance from contaminated equipment; developed under Project Vista (bridging SBIR/OTA-funded small business tech into fielded Army capability); potential Joint Force (Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps) applications noted.
- **2026-05-11:** Successfully demonstrated the eXpeditionary Cargo Loader (XCL) for US Air Force tactical airlift operations (C-130 cargo capacity increase) and secured a follow-on contract for additional units.
- **2025-04-15:** Selected by the US Air Force as part of the Enterprise-Wide Agile Acquisition Contract (EWAAC), a multiple-award IDIQ vehicle with a $46 billion ceiling running through 2031, covering weapons-related capability development.
- **2022-08-16:** Awarded a US Army contract to develop a standalone Perimeter Safety for Autonomous Vehicles (P-SAV) system for the Robotic Combat Vehicle-Light (RCV-L) program.
- **2009:** Received the DoD Mentor-Protégé Program's Nunn-Perry Award.

## Supply Chain Position

**Value Chain Layer:** Integrator with platform-OEM elements.
