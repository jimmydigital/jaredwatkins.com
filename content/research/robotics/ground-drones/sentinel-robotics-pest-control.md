## Summary

"Sentinel Robotics" is the social/marketing handle for an early-stage venture — pitched internally as "Project Sentinel" — building what it calls an "autonomous kinetic sentry": a detection-plus-effector turret aimed at feral hogs (and, per its bio, other invasive/pest species), positioned simultaneously as a biosecurity/agricultural tool and as a generator of "adversarial data" for future autonomous-combat applications. The X account is based in New Zealand and has, in 2026, run a Feb 28 investor pitch ("Project Sentinel"), an Apr 21 product update (a smaller .22-caliber "Sentinel mini" variant), a May 18 data-methodology thread modeling the US feral hog population, and a Jun 2026 farmer/rancher survey campaign — before the public-facing site reverted to an unlabeled "stealth mode" teaser by the time of this review. Pitch-deck imagery posted by the account brands the underlying entity "Arcane Design Works," which plausibly (but not confirmedly) corresponds to a Christchurch, New Zealand agri-robotics startup of that name listed on Wellfound. This is a distinct entity from the similarly-named Cluj-Napoca, Romania aerial UAV maker documented separately in this section (see [Sentinel Robotics — aerial UAVs]({{< relref "../aerial-drones/sentinel-robotics.md" >}})), and from the unrelated Wallops Island, Virginia-based "Sentinel Robotic Solutions" / "Sentinel Robotics Group" (Magpie aerial drone line, srsgrp.com).

## What It Is / How It Works

**The pitched product.** Per pitch-deck imagery posted to the account on February 28, 2026 (branded "ARCANE DESIGN WORKS — PROJECT SENTINEL V1.4"), the core product is described as an "Autonomous Kinetic Sentry — Phase 1 Prototype": a stationary or trailer-mounted turret combining a detection/tracking system with an electrically-actuated kinetic effector, pitched under the tagline "Biosecurity for the 21st Century. AI-driven lethal precision against the invasive species costing the US $4.7B+ annually — while building the adversarial datasets that will define autonomous combat tomorrow." That framing is explicitly dual-use: the stated business case is agricultural pest control, but the stated strategic value proposition to investors is the training-data byproduct for autonomous weapons applications. The deck cites 360° detection coverage and a 200 m detection range, "100% electric actuation," a 6–7 month timeline to TRL 6, and a $1M Phase 1 funding ask. A companion image shows a 3D-rendered turret unit — a camera/sensor head paired with a barrel-like effector on a rotating mount — alongside a still of shotgun-style shells laid out on grass, suggesting a shotgun-shell-based effector for the full-size unit.

On April 21, 2026, the account announced "Sentinel mini," described as "an air driven small pest control sentry featuring a belt fed, digitally controlled effector in .22 calibre and all the detection and tracking technology as the full fat Sentinel" — i.e., a smaller-caliber variant of the same detect-and-engage architecture, justified by the post's own reasoning that the original's larger effector ("12ga," per the post's phrasing) is oversized for small-pest engagements.

**The data/methodology thread.** Separately from the hardware pitch, the account has run a more substantive analytical effort. In a May 18, 2026 seven-part thread, it argued that the commonly cited "more than 6 million" US feral hog population figure is a decade-stale 2016 estimate, and walked through a specific recompute: citing Lewis et al. (2019) — described as the peer-reviewed paper USDA APHIS itself cites — for a 2016 baseline of 6.9 million and a habitat-saturation ceiling of up to 65.6 million (95% prediction interval), noting the commonly repeated "6M" figure drops that upper bound; citing APHIS's own published net annual growth rate of 18–21% under current control efforts, plus reproductive parameters (sows producing 1.5–2 litters/year, 4–6 piglets each, sexual maturity at 6–8 months); and computing 6.9M × 1.18¹⁰ ≈ 36.1 million as a lower-bound decade-compounded estimate using APHIS's own baseline and growth rate. The thread also sketched a seasonal model (bimodal farrowing peaks in February and May, an August trough from summer heat stress on piglets, and multi-year "mast year" boom cycles in oak-dominant range). This is a real, source-cited extrapolation — not an independent census or a peer-reviewed update — but it is a materially more rigorous methodology than the rest of the account's marketing content, and the underlying USDA/APHIS-attributed figures it cites (6.9M 2016 baseline, 18–21% growth rate, Lewis et al. 2019) were not independently re-verified against APHIS or the original paper in this session.

The account announced a "live counter" reflecting this model at sentinelrobotic.com on May 18, 2026, displaying a compounding population estimate (accompanying video showed "39,030,958," "LIVE ESTIMATE · COMPOUNDING AT 18%/YR") alongside a running agricultural-damage figure (video showed "$5,051,597,025... USD, CUMULATIVE SINCE 01.01.2026 · $300/HOG/YR"). Neither the counter nor the damage tracker is present on the live site as of this review (August 2026) — the site currently shows only a "stealth mode" teaser, a regression from its May 2026 state.

**The survey campaign.** In late June 2026, the account pivoted to soliciting primary data directly: a pinned post (June 27, 2026) promotes the "2026 Sentinel Hog Impact Survey," asking ranchers, farmers, and land owners to contribute firsthand data on feral hog impact, following an initial campaign post on June 22, 2026.
