## Summary

Boston Dynamics, based in Waltham, Massachusetts, makes the Spot quadruped, the Stretch trailer-unloading robot and the Atlas humanoid. Hyundai acquired a controlling stake from SoftBank in 2021 and Hyundai vice chair Jaehoon Chang chairs the board. On 2026-10-06 it named Rohit Prasad, formerly head scientist for Alexa and AGI at Amazon, as CEO effective the next day; he succeeds CFO Amanda McMaster, interim CEO since Robert Playter stepped down in February. Atlas production begins in 2026, with first units going to Hyundai's Robotics Metaplant Application Center (RMAC) and Google DeepMind and other customers expected in early 2027.

## Key Facts

- **HQ:** Waltham, MA; plans to spend $100M expanding facilities there
- **Ownership:** controlled by Hyundai (acquired from SoftBank, 2021)
- **CEO:** Rohit Prasad (from 2026-10-07; expected to join the board); third CEO after Marc Raibert (1992–2020) and Robert Playter
- **Board chair:** Jaehoon Chang, Hyundai vice chair
- **Atlas:** production begins 2026; 6.2 ft tall; 7.5 ft reach; payload up to 66 lb; IP67; operating range -4°F to 104°F; about four-hour battery, hot-swappable in roughly three minutes; fenceless operation via onboard people and vehicle detection; first customers Hyundai RMAC and Google DeepMind; others early 2027; actuators from Hyundai's Mobi platform; price not given
- **Atlas ROI claim:** company says most customers will see ROI within two years
- **Hyundai target:** about 30,000 robotics systems per year from a single factory (all products), tied to Hyundai's $26B US manufacturing investment
- **RMAC:** opened in Georgia at Hyundai Metaplant America to train Atlas integration
- **Spot 5.2:** adds agentic workflows; Orbit AIVI now powered by Google Gemini
- **Stretch:** trailer-unloading robot
- **Revenue:** not disclosed in sources reviewed

## What It Is / How It Works

Spot is a legged inspection robot, Stretch a box-handling arm on a mobile base, and Atlas a bipedal humanoid aimed first at autonomous material handling. The company says early Atlas use cases will be limited. Research deploying Gemini Robotics models on Atlas was set to start in early 2026. The new Atlas hand has four fingers and no pinky, designed for simulation and sim-to-real reinforcement learning.

**Leadership shift.** Prasad's stated plan is to productize intelligent robotic systems for industrial and commercial settings as part of a "physical AI" strategy; he spent 12 years at Amazon and nearly 14 at Raytheon BBN before that.
