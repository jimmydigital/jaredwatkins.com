## Summary

AMP Robotics Corp. (branded "AMP") is a Denver/Louisville, Colorado–based company applying computer-vision AI and robotic sortation arms to municipal recycling and waste-processing facilities (materials recovery facilities, or MRFs). Founded in 2014 by Matanya Horowitz, AMP has raised at least $91M+55M+91M+16M across four disclosed rounds (Series A through D) from investors including Sequoia Capital, Congruent Ventures, and XN, and has deployed more than 400 AI-guided sorting systems across North America, Asia, and Europe. In 2024 the company brought on Tim Stuart, former COO of Republic Services (NYSE: RSG), as CEO, with founder Horowitz moving to Chief Technology Officer.

## Key Facts

- **Type:** Private; Series D (as of Dec 2024)

- **Business model:** AI software + robotic hardware integrator/platform — sells retrofit sorting robots and, increasingly, fully integrated facility-scale sortation systems (AMP ONE)
- **Value Chain Layer:** Software/AI layer + integrator — builds its neural-network vision system in-house and integrates robotic arms and pneumatic (air-jet) sortation hardware into MRF-scale systems, rather than manufacturing general-purpose industrial arms itself
- **Deployment scale (company-reported):** 400+ AI systems deployed; AI platform has identified 150 billion items and guided sortation of 2.5 million+ tons of recyclables (as of Dec 2024)

## What It Is / How It Works

AMP's core technology is a two-part system: **AMP Neuron**, a deep-learning computer-vision platform that identifies recyclable materials and contaminants in a mixed waste stream in real time by recognizing colors, textures, shapes, sizes, and logos on high-speed conveyor lines; and **AMP Cortex** (plus the newer **Delta** robotic-arm sortation technology), the robotic hardware layer that physically picks identified items off the line — the company claims pick rates upward of 80 items per minute, more than double typical human sorter throughput, with greater consistency. AMP's product line has evolved from single retrofit robots installed into existing MRFs toward **AMP ONE**, a fully integrated, facility-scale "Smart Sortation" system with near-zero manual sorting, first proven on municipal solid waste (MSW) — a harder, less pre-sorted material stream than traditional single-stream recycling — at a facility in Portsmouth, Virginia. In November 2024, AMP entered an agreement with Waste Connections, Inc. (TSX/NYSE: WCN) to equip and operate a next-generation single-stream recycling facility in Commerce City, Colorado (62,000 tons/year capacity, commissioning targeted early 2026).

Within the Robotics section's value-chain framework, AMP sits primarily in the **software/AI layer plus integrator** category rather than as a traditional industrial-arm manufacturer (FANUC, ABB, KUKA, Yaskawa) — it builds proprietary perception software and system integration on top of off-the-shelf and custom robotic-arm and air-jet sortation hardware, similar in structural position to how autonomy-software companies sit atop hardware platforms elsewhere in the Robotics section.
