## Summary

Giga Energy is a Houston-based company that manufactures electrical infrastructure for data centers and combines it with powered-land origination and modular data center development. Its niche is the mid-voltage and distribution layer — three-phase padmount transformers, medium- and high-voltage substations, and low-voltage switchboards — the equipment class where data center projects are currently waiting longest. The company's own research blog argues that transformer and switchgear lead times, not capital or demand, now set energization dates, and it sells a shorter-lead-time alternative.

## Key Facts

- **Type:** Company — component/subsystem supplier with developer and operator arms (vertically integrated)
- **Value chain position:** Electrical equipment manufacturer (transformers, substations, switchboards, prefabricated power/cooling modules); site developer; colocation operator; demand-response market participant
- **Founded:** 2019, by Brent Whitehead and Matt Lohstroh (per the company's About page)
- **HQ:** Houston, TX (per the company's About page)
- **Manufacturing:** Houston, TX (transformers); Long Beach, CA (switchboards); supply chain also includes Mumbai facilities and a Shanghai office (company-stated)

- **Status:** Active; has said it intends to build toward public-company standards and an eventual IPO (Pulse 2.0 interview, Nov 2025)

## What It Is / How It Works

Giga Energy sells equipment from the utility interconnection down to the rack. The catalog listed on its site includes three-phase padmount transformers (up to 46 kV and 10,000 kVA), medium-voltage substations (up to 35 kV and 60 MVA), high-voltage substations (up to 138 kV and 100 MVA), UL891 low-voltage switchboards, 24.9/35 kV MV switchgear, a 3.0 MW lithium-ion UPS e-house, a 3.6 MVA power skid with 5,000A switchboards, a 3.3 MW diesel generator module, and a 2 MW air-cooled maglev chiller. Higher up the stack it offers GigaPod (a 45-foot AI compute module with air-to-liquid cooling and 4N/3 redundant power, scalable to 90 or 135 feet) and GigaBase (a turnkey modular AI data center advertised as built in 9 months).

The business model bundles four things: manufacturing, powered land (sites with high-voltage infrastructure and fiber), site development (build-to-suit on its own or the customer's land), and colocation for high-density GPU deployments. A fifth arm, Giga Power Systems, enrolls flexible-load sites in demand response and energy markets. A separate legacy line sells containerized bitcoin-mining units (Giga Box Hydro and Air).

**Lead-time thesis.** A Giga Energy blog post on data center lead-time delays frames equipment procurement as a schedule constraint rather than a line item, and argues that many project pro formas understate it by relying on blended averages. Key figures it cites (several attributed to Wood Mackenzie, with others from EPC and developer quotes shared with the author):

| Equipment | Lead time cited |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Three-phase padmount transformers (data center class) | 40+ weeks (EPC/developer quotes; blended published averages understate this class) |
| Average distribution transformers | ~30 weeks (Wood Mackenzie), down from a 2023 peak above 100 weeks |
| Power transformers | ~128 weeks average |
| Generator step-up transformers | ~144 weeks average; some specialized orders up to four years |
| Switchgear (published average) | 44 weeks |
| Standard switchboards (data center class, contractor reporting) | ~52 weeks |
| Power circuit breaker switchboards | 84+ weeks |
| Medium-voltage switchgear | Reportedly approaching two to three years |

The post also reports demand growth since 2019 of about 77% for three-phase padmount units (vs. about 30% for pole-top), about 119% for power transformers, and about 274% for generator step-up units, plus price increases of about 77% for power transformers and 78–95% for distribution transformers. It identifies three overlapping demand drivers — data center load (citing LBNL's projection of 325–580 TWh of US data center consumption by 2028, up from 176 TWh in 2023), replacement of aging distribution transformers (more than half of roughly 40 million in service are past expected life), and wider electrification — and notes heavy import reliance (roughly 80% of US power transformer supply and 50% of distribution transformer supply came from abroad in 2025). Its recommended playbook: break energization into milestones so the equipment order date surfaces early, screen sites on equipment reality (substation configuration, spare bays, serving-circuit transformer age), release equipment orders before full project certainty, and choose manufacturers that control their own queues. The post is marketing content from an equipment seller; its framing favors the vendor's own product.

**Capacity.** Giga's homepage states 1,000+ transformers of annual capacity in Houston. A December 2025 announcement described a new 60,000 sq ft Houston medium-voltage transformer plant with capacity above 3,000 units per year (15 GW annually), with production expected to start in Q1 2026.

## Supply Chain Position

Giga Energy sits at the distribution and medium-voltage layer, downstream of large power transformer makers such as [Hitachi Energy]({{< relref "hitachi-energy.md" >}}) and alongside prefabricated power vendors such as [Vertiv]({{< relref "vertiv.md" >}}). Its pitch is that the padmount and MV class — not only the >100 MVA units that dominate shortage coverage — is a binding constraint for campuses energizing in the next two years, and that a domestic manufacturer controlling its own queue can shorten it. The company lists CoreWeave, Vultr, Supermicro, CleanSpark, Soluna, Fluidstack, Black Creek and WESCO as logos on its site without describing the relationships.

**⚑ Shared bottleneck:** Giga's padmount transformers and switchboards draw on the same constrained inputs (electrical steel, copper, skilled winders, test capacity) and the same import-exposed supply base as the large power transformer shortage tracked in the [Power Infrastructure overview]({{< relref "../power-infrastructure/_index.md" >}}).
